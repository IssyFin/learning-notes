<summary>The American System</summary>

- Context: Weaknesses were revealed by the War of 1812
  - No National bank or transportation system-difficult to raise funds and move soldiers and supplies
- Proposed by Henry Clay and John Calhoun in 1815 to strengthen the US economy
- 3 parts
  - Create a new national bank
  - Pass a protective tariff, encouraging purchase of American made goods
  - Use federal money to build and improve roads and canals
- National bank created and tariff passed in 1816,
- Resistance
  - Madison vetoed infrastructure funding, fearing expansion of federal power
  - White southerners worries that push for industry would strengthen the north
  - Southern fear of the eradication of slavery led to a push for regional interests, including Calhoun’s growing support for the industry of slavery
- Regional interests over unity
  - The question of slavery
    - Missouri Compromise led Missouri to join the union as a slave state and Maine to join as a free state and set the 36°30’ parallel
  - Nullification crisis
    - South Carolina declared they'd ignore tariffs of 1828 and 1832, and considered they helped northern regions and hurt southern farmers
    - President Jackson passed the force bill, allowing him to use military to enforce the tariff

</details>

<details>
<summary>The Market Revolution</summary>

- The Market Revolution (1790-1850)
  - Industrial Revolution
    - Huge improvements and innovations in the kinds of machinery used to make finished goods
    - Initial US imagined as a united nation of small farmers → became nation of hourly laborers who used those wages to buy what they needed
    - Onslaught of new inventions
      - Samuel Slater: Englishman who worked in a textile mill. UK previously the world capital of textiles
        - Illegal to export plans for a textile mill - but Slater thought it wasn’t illegal to export his brain, and memorized how the mills and looms worked (water-wheel powered)
      - Francis Cabot Lowell starts many textile mills in Rhode Island, employing lots of young women, who were looking for independence (and whom they could pay less money)
        ![](https://bethkeushistory.weebly.com/uploads/1/1/6/2/1162327/4773009.jpg?353x223)
      - Late 1840s: Howe makes a *nice* sewing machine (better than current versions), later improved by Singer - massive textile mills become backbone of New England commerce
      - Cotton Gin: 1793 (Eli Whitney): Box with spikes that separates balls of cotton from seeds; previously very labor intensive → a single person can process 50 pounds of cotton in a day
        - Institution of slavery started to die out before the introduction of the cotton gin, which made slavery more profitable again
    - Movement towards individual work vs family unit work, makes work for women more respectable

</details>

<details>
<summary>Communication and Transportation in the Market Revolution</summary>

- Inventions allowed faster transportation of goods and information
- Telegraph - Samuel Morse (1844)
  - Sent pulses down copper wires - easy to communicate over long distance via coded messages
  - Very rapid movement of information over long distances
  - Can now more easily do business overseas
  - Much more rapid pace of business
- Railroad
  - Technology imported from England and Germany in early 1800s
  - Helped with move stone for canals
  - Later passenger movement
    - B&O (Baltimore and Ohio) railroad: one of the first passenger rail lines in the US
- Water shipping: Steamship (1807) (Robert Fulton)
  - Allows movement against river tide
  - Erie Canal - 1825: narrow, shallow waterway that can navigate barges

</details>

<details>
<summary>Impact and Significance of the Market Revolution</summary>

- Labor
  - Movement to individual, factory labor from family farm basis
    - Wages ← Barter Economy
    - People no longer their own bosses/in charge of themselves
    - Less control over daily life, can’t set pace of own life
  - Interchangeable parts: Fewer artisans/masters of craft can make goods start-to-finish
    - Less ownership of/pride in work - eg, master cobbler → nails in shoes
    - Deskilling of workforce
      - No need for “highly-trained artisans” (and less need to pay or treat well - strikers can get fired) and puts labor force in more precarious position
- Entry into the international market system
  - Market economy ← Mercantalism
    - Previously, US just shipped raw materials to Europe for processing
    - War of 1812: US embargoes England and invests in own factories
  - Room for speculation, boom/bust, transition to capitalism and investing
    - Panic of 1819: first “bubble burst” economic event
    - Less control over the market - overproduction, consumer hesitance, etc
    - Led to Jackson’s obsession w/ a national bank
  - US dependence on slavery to be a world economic power in cotton
- Second Great Awakening
  - Explosion of religious fervor, suspected due to the anxious forces associated with the labor and economical changes
  - Less control → need new explanations and comfort for a confusing world

</details>

<details>
<summary>Immigration and the market revolution</summary>

- Primary immigrant groups in the early 1800s: German and Irish
- Irish Immigrants
  - Massive influx of Irish people due to the potato famine in the 1840s
  - Immigrants faced fierce discrimination
    - Irish Catholics especially faced pushback; many Americans were strongly anti Catholic - rumors that they were superstitious, blindly loyal to the pope, and could not be loyal americans
      ![Engraving showing the 1844 riots in Philadelphia, when mobs attacked Irish Catholics and destroyed St. Augustine Church. Public domain.](https://cdn.kastatic.org/ka-content-images/9ada295a741f3d212f2f6476c0062f730fec8a68.jpg)
    - **Immigrant life**
      - **Many immigrant communities involved themselves deeply in politics**
        - **Grew organizations to assist immigrants with finding housing and work, supported associated political machines (eg Tammany Hall and William Tweed in NY)**
  - Community Life
    - Irish communities supported organizations that helped immigrants find jobs, housing, and community support
    - Many Irish people also joined political machines, specifically Tammany Hall in NYC, led by William “Boss” Tweed
      - Controlled NY politics from 1850s-1870s before he was arrested for embezzlement, corruption, and fraud
  - Became one of the most successful, prosperous, and well-educated immigrant groups in the US by the mid-1900s
- German Immigrants
  - Largest immigrant group in the country by 1848
    - Sought political freedom, religious liberty, and economic opportunity not available in Europe
    - 1848: Series of revolutions in German states prompted many Germans to come to America
  - Brought and developed technologies
    - Crop rotation, soil conservation
    - Pursued education, started businesses, and engaged in commerce

</details>
