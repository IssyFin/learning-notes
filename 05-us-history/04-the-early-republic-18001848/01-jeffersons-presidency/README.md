### The Revolution of 1800 (1800)

*Historical Events · American · [Notion](https://app.notion.com/p/30944de2a656806f832fc3fcad032d27)*

**Sources**

- [Khan article on the Revolution of 1800](https://www.khanacademy.org/humanities/us-history/the-early-republic/politics-society-early-19th-c/a/the-revolution-of-1800)

<!-- page-notes:30944de2a656806f832fc3fcad032d27 -->
#### Page notes

**Seeds of Division**

- The first two parties
  - Conflict between John Adams’s (Federalists) and Thomas Jefferson’s (Democratic-Republicans) ideals
  - Federalists
    - Strong central economy, cities, trade, manufacturing
  - Democratic Republicans
    - Ideal of small, independent farmers focused on hard work ethic, independence, and civic virtue
- Other items of division
  - How should US respond to the French Revolution?
    - Democratic Republicans supported France - saw that struggle as a continuation of the quest for liberty
    - Federalists feared that violent tendencies would spread to US, and supported Great Britain
  - Alien and Sedition Acts (signed into law by Adams in 1798)
    - Made it harder for immigrants to become citizens
    - Allowed government to punish those who criticized federal government officials
    - Democratic Republicans believed that it violated the First Amendment tight to free speech and that it was meant to silence Adams’s political opponents
  - Federalist party split between John Adams and Alexander Hamilton
- Election of 1800: Filled with fierce attacks and thorny divisions

**The Revolution of 1800**

- Election of 1800
  - Stretched over several months since each state could set its own voting day
  - Very tight race between Adams and Jefferson
  - Complicated results
    - Jefferson and his running mate (Aaron Burr) received an equal number of electoral votes. No separate ballot for vp, so House of Representatives (controlled by Federalists) got to decide. Most federalists preferred Burr, but Hamilton, wary of Burr’s ambition, convinced them to choose Jefferso instead.
  - Tension continued; Adams wouldn’t even shake Jefferson’s hand at the inauguration
- Deepened political divisions
  - After Adams’s loss, Federalists quickly lost influence
- Marked the first peaceful transition of power between parties

### Thomas Jefferson (1801–1809)

US President (Democratic-Republican)  
Founding Father  
Advocacy for individual rights

**Sources**

- [Khan Article on the Jeffersonian Republic](https://www.khanacademy.org/humanities/us-history/the-early-republic/politics-society-early-19th-c/a/the-jeffersonian-republic)

<!-- page-notes:22444de2a656807a8417ee35623593bc -->
#### Page notes

- Context
  - One of America’s founding fathers
  - Third president of US, marking first peaceful transition of power between rival political parties
  - Secretary of State under Washington, VP under Adams
- Focuses while in office
  - Reduce national debt from earlier administrations
  - After cutting the navy to save funds, had to rebuild the navy due to piracy
- The First Barbary War (1801-1805)
  - After US split from Great Britain, US had to defend its own ships
  - Pirates from along the coast of Barbary (northern Africa) attacked American ships traveling to Europe; enslaved crews, stole cargo, ransomed crews
    ![A map of the northern coast of Africa bordering the Mediterranean Sea, known as the Barbary Coast. Public domain.](https://cdn.kastatic.org/ka-perseus-images/7ca60b7b9ab2060e4b149d4b4099e6faf1af8b5a.jpg)
  - Ruler of Tripoli demanded US pay \$225,000 tribute to prevent future attacks on American ships
    - Jefferson refused, and Tripoli declared war on the US
  - Jefferson sent the navy to the Mediterranean, attacking Tripoli; leader of Tripoli ceded and signed a peace treaty
  - Short war, but demonstrated that the US could fight and win wars far from home
    ![Painting depicting a ship raid near Tripoli by Dennis Malone Carter, early 1800s. Public domain.](https://cdn.kastatic.org/ka-perseus-images/302182265ae2b060026d00f0a31c190db1444b65.jpg)
- The Embargo Act of 1807
  - Context: France and Britain were fighting the Napoleonic Wars from 1803-1815; both nations seized American ships to prevent them from trading with the enemy
    - British Royal Navy also imprisoned US sailors, forcing them to serve on British Warships (impressment)
  - 1807: British Warship, the *HMS Leopard*, attacked a US ship, the *USS Chesapeake*, off the coast of Norfolk, VA; British fired on the ship and captured American sailors
  - Jefferson passed the Embargo Act of 1807, preventing all American ships from leaving US ports until Britain and France agreed to stop attacking them
    - Hoped that cutting off trade would add economic pressure
    - Embargo hurt US far more than the international trading partners
      - US trade dropped 75%
      - Seaport jobs depleted
      - Farmers couldn’t ship crops
    - Difficult to enforce, especially since Americans could smuggle goods to Canada for shipment
  - Jefferson passed the Non-Intercourse Act of 1808, ending the Embargo Act of 1807 (while maintaining restrictions for Britain and France)
- [The Louisiana Purchase](https://app.notion.com/p/30944de2a65680468a0be06912e8cc68)
  - The US under Jefferson purchased 800,000 square miles of land from France, doubling the US territory

### The Louisiana Purchase (1803–1806)

*Historical Events · American · [Notion](https://app.notion.com/p/30944de2a65680468a0be06912e8cc68)*

US doubled in size after the US under Jefferson purchased 800,000 square miles of land from France, from Mississippi River to the Rocky Mountains

**Sources**

- Khan Article on the Louisiana Purchase

<!-- page-notes:30944de2a65680468a0be06912e8cc68 -->
#### Page notes

- Context
  - Land passed between France and Spain several times before the Louisiana Purchase
    - 1800: Spain returned the land to France, at the time ruled by Napoleon Bonaparte
  - Napoleon, focused on wars in Europe, found it too difficult to manage land in the Americas; offered to sell all 823,000 square miles to the US for \$15 million
  ![](https://cdn.kastatic.org/ka-perseus-images/f2e229df2570357ca5d9a389ef9eb3ea50f87258.svg)
- Some hurdles in the deal
  - Interpretation of the Constitution
    - Jefferson wasn’t sure if the Constitution allowed him to purchase territory
      - Strict interpretation: Only what was specifically stated was allowed
  - Opposition by Federalists, who believed it might lead to creation of more slave states, which would shift the balance of Congress
- Lewis and Clark Expedition (1803-1806)
  - Captain Meriwether Lewis and Second Lieutenant William Clark of the US Army led the expedition to plot the new land
  - Sacagawea, a Shoshone woman, acted as their guide and interpreter
  ![](https://cdn.kastatic.org/ka-perseus-images/cffeff288a3659647314351ecdc161c6a602101f.jpg)
  - During the expedition, they documented plant and animal life, minerals, and more - greatly expanding scientific knowledge of the continent
