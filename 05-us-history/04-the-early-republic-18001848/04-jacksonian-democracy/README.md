### Jacksonian Democracy (1810–1850)

*Historical Periods · American · [Notion](https://app.notion.com/p/32744de2a656801f8eb2c48352ccfadd)*

**Sources**

#### Page notes

- Overview
  - Birth of the modern American political culture - lots of today’s practices emerged
    - 2 party system
    - Spoils system
    - Political character/traits we consider “electable”
- Prior to Jacksonian Democracy
  - Aristocratic - several families tended to dominate politics (eg Jeffersons, Washingtons, Adams…) - considered a “higher character of man”
    - Typically wealthy and educated
    - Virginia, Massachusetts… w/ exception of John Adams, every US president before 1824 was a rich slaveholder from Virginia
      - Expected to lead others and believed that people like them were naturally fit to governm
  - Aristocrats concerned that there could be “too much democracy” - worried about the “tyranny of the mob”
    - Considered to have stake if a property owner - need enough “stake” in the country to justify getting to have a say in it
    - Many states had restrictions on voting to just propertied men.
      - This moved on to just be white men, regardless of whether they paid taxes/owned property
      - Other states moved on to grant the right to vote to all white men. Until then, technically, women and men of color could vote if they owned property (\~1850s)
- Politics of the Common Man
  - Growth in voting rights and participation
    - Jacksonian Democracy refers to the growth in voting rights - Jackson’s election in 1828 came to represent the “politics of the common man”
    - Though less tied to wealth, became more restricted by race and gender; voting rights given to all white men, but taken away from African Americans and women who had property
    - Very high voter participation: in 1840, 79% of eligible voters cast a ballot in the presidential race
    - Voting became a form of public entertainment - huge crowds at debates, rallies, and parades.
    - Features of two-party system emerged: organized political parties, national conventions, partisan newspapers, campaign “mudslinging”between candidates
  - Rise of Political Parties: The Democrats and the Whigs
    - Federalist Party faded after the War of 1812, and Democratic-Republicans remained in power.
      - Little partisan conflict - the Era of Good Feelings
    - Election of 1824 hotly contested:
      - John Quincy Adams won after vote in the House of Representatives, but Andrew Jackson had more popular support
    - Andrew Jackson’s followers, angered by outcome of election of 1824, organized for the election of 1828, building the Democratic Party
      - United small farmers, city workers, immigrants, and Catholics - considered themselves the honest, hardworking people of the nation
      - Distrusted bankers, merchants, and large business owners
      - Valued individual freedom, opposed government moral reforms like temperence
    - Jacksonian critics formed the Whigs
      - Whigs wanted strong national government to growt the economy
      - Supported the American System (national bank protective tariffs, improvements to canals and railroads
      - Typically wealthier groups (business owners, bankers, large-scale farmers) and Protestant reformers who believed government could improve society
    - Second Party system lasted about 1828 until the 1850s (slavery debates split the Whigs)

### John Quincy Adams (1825–1829)

*Historical Figures · American · [Notion](https://app.notion.com/p/22444de2a65680f59dbef0e496587edf)*

![John Quincy Adams](https://upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/3/3a/JQA_Photo_Crop_%28cropped%29.jpg/800px-JQA_Photo_Crop_%28cropped%29.jpg)

US President (Democratic-Republican, National Republican)  
Helped negotiate end of War of 1812 as part of delegation, helped formulate Monroe Doctrine

Pushed for federally funded infrastructure, national university, engagement with Latin America

Spurred the National Republican Party (against Jackson’s Democratic Party)

- [Khan Article on the Presidency of John Quincy Adams](https://www.khanacademy.org/humanities/us-history/the-early-republic/x71a94f19:foreign-policy-in-the-early-1800s/a/the-presidency-of-john-quincy-adams-ush)

#### Page notes

- Background
  - Family was active in politics
  - Secretary of State under Monroe
- Actions
  - Helped solidify Treaty of Ghent in 1812
  - Worked with Britain to set the US-Canada border
  - Helped arrange the acquisition of Florida from Spain
  - Drafted the Monroe Doctrine
- The Election of 1824
  - Hotly contested, ran between Henry Clay, John Adams, and William Crawford
  - No candidate won a majority of electoral votes; House of Representatives decided the election
    - Jackson won the most popular and electoral votes
    - House of Representatives chose John Quincy Adams
  - Henry Clay voiced support for John Adams; John Adams later appointed Henry Clay as the speaker of the House, known to be a stepping stone to the presidency
    - Considered to be a “corrupt bargain”; regardless of whether the claim was true or not, sullied Adams’s reputation and allowed Jackson to pose himself as the “ordinary” man against the political elite

![](https://cdn.kastatic.org/ka-perseus-images/2a9b72d6b3a572bfb8cde488d7a77bd6253b3e65.jpg)

- The American System: Strengthen the economy with government support
  - Improve trade and transportation by building roads, bridges, and canals
  - Protect American industry with tariffs
  - Maintain national bank and stable currency
  - Reduce national debt from 16 million to 5 million
- Decline in popularity
  - Sympathy towards Native Americans was unpopular with many Americans
  - 1828: Approved tariff called “the Tariff of Abominations” by South -
- Post-Presidency
  - Lost presidency to Jackson in 1828
  - Career in public service
    - Represented Massachusetts in the House of Representatives for seventeen years
  - One of the strongest voices in Congress opposing slavery
    - During Mexican-American war, warned that annexing Texas could lead to national conflict over slavery - which it did, in the Civil War
    - 1841: Argued on behalf of enslaved peoples who took over the Spanish ship *Amistad;* Court ruled in their favor, declaring them free

### Andrew Jackson (1829–1835)

![Andrew Jackson](https://upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/2/2a/Andrew_jackson_head_%28cropped%29.jpg/800px-Andrew_jackson_head_%28cropped%29.jpg)

US President (Democratic)

Preserve state union, racist policies towards Native Americans and enslaved people,  led wars for land (eg Battle of Horseshoe Bend in Creek War, Battle of New Orleans, First Seminole War)

Indian Removal Act, ethnic cleansing, Trail of Tears

Dismantled Second Bank of the US, vetoing Congress

Criticized for ignoring the law when it suited him

Only president to pay off the national debt

#### Page notes

- The Election of 1824 (won by John Quincy Adams) - The Corrupt Bargain
  - Context: Expansion of Democracy
    - Change of voting requirements: now all white male citizens could vote
    - Movement to common man politics
      - Previously, “standing” for office rather than “running” for office - kind of understood who would be taking over
  - Election results
    - Andrew Jackson wins the popular vote
    - John Quincy Adams (son of John Adams) wins the electoral vote
      - Represents the “old election style”
      - Wealthy, educated, didn’t consider himself part of a “party” - kind of just expected he’d lead
    - Henry Clay, Speaker of the House (and in the race against Adams and Jackson), gets House to vote for Adams over Jackson, “supposedly” for the position of Secretary of State, but no evidence - Called a corrupt bargain by Jackson’s followers
      - Even if no evidence, followed the old playbook of doing things
- The Election of 1828 (won by Jackson)
  - Outrage over election outcome led to organization for the election of 1828
  - Growth of mudslinging
    - Corrupt bargain used as fodder on Jackson’s behalf
    - Critics of Jackson distributed “coffin handbills” to show how many people died to Jackson either during the war, during duels, etc
  - Mass-Party Democracy:
    - Jackson has great party machines in eastern cities
    - Frontier - white people looking to expand westward
  - Jackson party use of anti-Indian sentiment
  - Immediate different in culture - new system - felt like anarchy to the aristocrats
    - Jackson bows - considers himself beneath those who he is serving
    - Opens the whitehouse up to the public - they break china and furniture
  - Signaled beginning of new politics, open to the common man
- Andrew Jackson’s background
  - Born to a poor Scots-Irish immigrant family (stark contrast to aristocratic presidential candidates at the time)
  - Served as a messenger for the patriots during the Revolution, and was captured by the British; while imprisoned, was injured and caught smallpox
  - After the war, studied law and became wealthy, then entered politics in Tennessee
  - Known for his temper and his sense of honor
  - Became a hero during the War of 1812; led troops to victor over the Creek Nation. During his 1817 campaign against the Native Americans, led attack on the Seminole and Creek populations and led Spain to cede Florida to the US
- Andrew Jackson as President
  - Fought a “war” against the national bank
    - Believed it only helped rich businessowners of the Northeast. Bank’s president,Nicholas Biddle, argued that it kept the economy stable.
    - Supreme court ruled the Bank as constitutional but Jackson still considered it a threat to democracy
    - Separated the national bank by transferring funds to smaller, state banks, which contributed to the recession of 1837
  - Policies and positions severely harmed Native Americans
    - Believed Native Americans stood in the way of white expansion and forced movement to the west of the Mississippi via the Indian Removal Act
    - Resulting forced migration of Native Americans became known as the Trail of Tears
    - Those who resisted were often attacked by US soldiers or settlers
  - Tried to expand power of the executive branch over the other branches
    - Liberal use of the veto
    - Often called “King Andrew I”
  - Tariffs
    - Prior to Jackson’s presidency, tariffs were put in place following the War of 1812 to protect Northern industry; price of British goods were inexpensive and the Northern factories needed a way to compete.
    - Southerners (including Jackson) considered these to be the “Tariff of Abominations”
    - 1832: Jackson signed into law a reduction of tariff rates, but not enough for South Carolina leaders - “nullified” the tariffs and threatened to secede if they were enforced
      - Nullification Crisis background
        - Question of whether States could nullify or cancel a federal law within its borders if its court deemed it unconstitutional
        - John Quincy Adams argued that only the federal government could determine if a law was unconstitutional
        - Jackson sympathized with Southerners but considered that nullification could threaten the union; supported states rights but drew the line at disunion
      - Jackson signed a compromise into law - reduced tariffs but also allowed the federal government to use the military to enforce tariffs if needed
  - Began the modern democratic system (Jacksonian democracy)
    - Initiated the Spoils System, where positions in power were rewarded to party followers/supporters
    - Began the push for the “common man” in positions of power (or, representing them as such, even if that wasn’t necessarily the case)

### Martin Van Buren (1837–1841)

*Historical Figures · American · [Notion](https://app.notion.com/p/22444de2a65680019bf4e89c286a1acc)*

![Martin Van Buren](https://upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/1/1a/Martin_Van_Buren.jpg/800px-Martin_Van_Buren.jpg)

US President (Democratic)

Panic of 1837 (depression with low wages, prices, high unemployment, and pessimism) led to the Independent Treasury System - federalgovt to store its funds in vaults rather than banks.

Refusal to admit Texas to the Union as a slave state. Later an anti-slavery leader.

Fun facts:  
Only president to have spoken English as a second language

### John Tyler (1841–1845)

*Historical Figures · American · [Notion](https://app.notion.com/p/22444de2a65680efaa35d4ba6cc08f90)*

![John Tyler](https://upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/9/99/Tyler_Daguerreotype_%28restoration%29_%28cropped%29.jpg/800px-Tyler_Daguerreotype_%28restoration%29_%28cropped%29.jpg)

US President (Whig)  
Succeeded to presidency following William Henry Harrison’s Death

Supporter of states’ rights, including regarding slavery. Only adopted nationalistic policies when they didn’t infringe on states’ powers.

Stalemates on Domestic policy, but did make a treaty with Britain and a Treaty with China.

Believer in Manifest Destiny (settlers destined to expand westward) and wanted to annex Texas

Fun Fact:

Almost his entire cabinet resigned shortly into his term, and his own party dubbed him “His Accidency”.

First president to have his veto of legislation overridden by Congress

### William Henry Harrison (1841)

*Historical Figures · American · [Notion](https://app.notion.com/p/22444de2a65680e39f1df868fcfe9a13)*

![William Henry Harrison](https://upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/2/2c/William_Henry_Harrison_crop.jpg/800px-William_Henry_Harrison_crop.jpg)

US President (Whig)

Died in office after only one month

Nominated via “Tippecanoe and Tyler Too”, focus on Indian treaties and acquiring title to Indian lands to allow for future settlement. Focused on Indiana’s eventual statehood, and did so by paying Native American chiefs with alcohol and funds

Held a pro-slavery position, tried to encourage slavery in Indiana. Lobbied Congress to suspend Article VI of Northwest Ordinance prohibiting slavery in the territory

Fun facts:  
Died in office before presidential succession was defined constitutionally
