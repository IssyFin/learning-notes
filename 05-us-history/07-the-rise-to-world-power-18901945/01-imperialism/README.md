## The End of Isolationism

### Context

- Post-Revolution Isolationism: cautious about involving in world affairs; wars were dangerous, alliances could pull US into wars
- 1870-1890: Colonization of Asia and Africa by Great Britain, France, Germany, and Japan
  - Fear that if US didn't join, it would fall behind other world powers
  - <img width="845" height="507" alt="image" src="https://github.com/user-attachments/assets/b537d468-3deb-443b-969c-1688a5644f0d" />
- Economic benefits of colonies: raw materials (rubber, oil, timber), new markets for manufactured goods
- US was reaching the end of westward growth; 1893, historian Frederick Jackson Turner argued that the American Frontier was closed

### US Becomes an Empire

- US Involvement in Spanish-American War
  - Cuban revolution - fight for independence from Spain
  - US Warship exploded in Havana Harbor and US declares war on Spain
  - Spanish-American War was relatively short; US defeated Spain and took possession of several territories: Puerto Rico, Guam, Philippines, and annexed Hawaii
- Debates over expansion
  - Some Americans believed this annexation went against American values, especially considering the US was once a colony itself; formed Anti-Imperialist League
  - Other Americans believed US had a duty to spread democracy, Christianity, and American values; believed in racial and cultural superiority of Americans of European descent
    - The "white man's burden"
  - Other expansion goals:
    - Build Navy
    - Build canal in central America

### Spanish-American War (1898)

#### Timeline

- 1893: American Plantation owners helped overthrow Queen Lili'uokalani, asked US to annex the islands
  - US Government first refused
- 1895: Cuban rebels began major uprising against Spain
  - Spain forces many Cubans into reconcentration camps to relocate rural populations away from rebels; poor living conditions and disease
- Exaggerated and false journaling anger AMericans about Spanish cruelty
- 1898: US sends battleship USS Maine to Havana Harbor to protect American citizens and property; the ship explodes, and (though Spain declares the explosion was accidental) US declares war on Spain

#### Overview 

- Most battling done on warships (US had fleet superiority) but many troops fought on land in Cuba and in the Philippines
  - Rough Riders: Led by Theodore Roosevelt, success of his unit depended heavily on experienced African American soldiers
  - US loses only ~400 soldiers in battle (though many more due to malaria and yellow fever) and Spain loses thousands; US wins and annexes acquired territory

#### Consequences

- US and Spain sign Treaty of Paris; Spain gives up Cuba and gives Puerto Rico and Guam to the US; US Pays $20 million to Spain for the Philippines
  - Note that neither Cubans nor Filipinos were included in negotiations
- Cuba independence limited under the Platt Amendment: Cuba had to allow US military and political intervention and lease Guantanamo Bay to the US
- Philippines didn't get independence at all; US believed Filipinos were not ready for self-government. Filipinos later rebelled leading to the Philippine-American War, which lasted until 1901, when Philippines became a US territory

#### Effects of the Spanish-American War

- US took control of Philippines, leading to resentment among Filipinos and the Philippine-American War (1899-1902)
- Americans became more involved in Asia; Philippines gave a foothold in the area
  - Secretary Hay's Open Door Policy - maintain American trade interests in China

### William McKinley (1897–1891)

*Historical Figures · American · [Notion](https://app.notion.com/p/22944de2a6568072b5d9c729c5130fe2)*

![William McKinley](https://upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/6/6d/Mckinley.jpg/500px-Mckinley.jpg)

US President (Republican)

Led US in Spanish-American Aar, oversaw expansionism and annexed Hawaii, Puerto Rico, Guam, Philippines, and American Samoa

Proposed that high tariffs would restore prosperity, signed Dingley Tariff of 1897 to protect manufacturers from foreign competition, passed Gold Standard Act of 1900

Foreign policy emulated era’s overseas imperialism of the great powers in Oceania, Asia, and the Caribbean Sea. Declared war on Spain over granting independence to rebellious Cuba; US saw quick and decisive victory and US won Puerto Rico, Guam, and Philippines colonies while Cuba was promised independence. Philippines pushed for independence but US suppressed it
