### Jim Crow (1877–1954)

*Historical Periods · American · [Notion](https://app.notion.com/p/2d944de2a6568070883bd3c702cb08ce)*

Effectively a “second-class citizenship” experience in the United States for African Americans, including different voting rights, separate and unequal public spaces, all practiced legally and encoded in law.

**Sources**

- [Khan video on the origins of Jim Crow](https://www.khanacademy.org/humanities/us-history/the-gilded-age/south-after-civil-war/v/jim-crow-part-1)

#### Page notes

- The Origins of Jim Crow
  - Jim Crow: not the name of a specific person, but of a “stock character” in the “minstrel show”
    - Minstrel show: Vaudeville-type performance popular in the north in 1830s-1840s
    - Jim Crow - the “stupid slave” who lived on the plantation, typically played by a white person in blackface
    - Became a derogatory term for people of African descent
  - Came from a long historical context:
    - 1620: Slaves imported to grow cotton and other cash crops
    - 1863: Emancipation Proclamation; race relations changed legally, so white people used different systems (systemically and ideologically) to fill the void and believe themselves superior
    - 1867: Civil war ends → Reconstruction
    - 1896: Plessy v Ferguson: Segregation legalized throughout the entire US by Supreme Court Ruling
    - 1947: Jackie Robinson broke the color-line in baseball by signing with the Brooklyn Dodgers
    - 1948: President Truman issued an executive order desegregating the US armed forces
    - 1954: Plessy v Ferguson overturned via Brown v Board of Education of Topeka
      - Supreme Court ruled that segregated facilities were inherently unequal
- Symbol for Racial Segregation
  - Examples
    - “Whites-only” restaurants, public schools
    - Illegal for Black Americans to sit at the front of public buses
  - Social dimensions
    - African Americans expected to demonstrate subservience and inferiority to whites at all times
      - Breaking these social norms commonly resulted in Black-owned businesses being burned to the ground, African Americans being fired from their places of work, and public lynchings
  - Voting and Legal Disenfranchisement- legal barriers to political, economic, and social success
    - Black men prevented from voting by means of the poll tax, literacy tests
    - Relegated to lower-paying jobs
    - Schools much more poorly funded

### Chester A. Arthur (1881–1885)

*Historical Figures · American · [Notion](https://app.notion.com/p/22944de2a65680cb86bdce428a9865e8)*

![Chester A. Arthur](https://upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/0/01/Chester_A._Arthur_by_Abraham_Bogardus.jpg/500px-Chester_A._Arthur_by_Abraham_Bogardus.jpg)

US President (Republican)

Assumed presidency following Garfield’s assassination

Largest expansion of the US Navy, the end of the spoils system (politicians get to award positions to friends and family as a winners perk), and harsher restrictions for migrants entering from abroad

Criticized for failing to alleviate the federal budget surplus accumulating since the Civil war

Vetoed the Chinese Exclusion Act (which would ban Chinese immigrants for 20 years) but signed second version which had a ten-year ban

Enforced Immigration Act of 1882 to impose restrictions on immigrants and Tariff of 1883 to reduce tariffs

Signed the Pendleton Civil Service Reform Act (federal ositions should be awarded on merit and not as a political win perk)

### Grover Cleveland (1885–1897)

*Historical Figures · American · [Notion](https://app.notion.com/p/22944de2a656803a870ce03f15d08756)*

![Grover Cleveland](https://upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/3/36/StephenGroverCleveland.jpg/500px-StephenGroverCleveland.jpg)

US President (Democratic)

Led bourbon Democrats (pro-business movement opposing high tariffs, free silver, inflation, imperialism, and subsidies to businesses, farmers, or veterans). Political reform and fiscal conservativism; icon for American conservatives.

Interstate Commerce Act of 1887 made railroad industry the first industry subject to federal regulation by a regulatory body

Dawes act - subdivided Native American tribal communal landholdings into individual allotments, which led Nataive Americans to cede control of 2/3 of their land from 1887-1934

Opposed push to annex Hawaii and investigated 1893 coup against Queen Lili’uokalani and called for her restoration

Intervened in 1894 Pullman strike to keep railroads moving, angering democrats and labor unions

Supported gold standard vs free silver

Protested 1898 Spanish-American War

Fun fact:  
Convinced many like-minded Republicans (mugwumps) to cross party lines

### Benjamin Harrison (1889–1893)

Sherman Antitrust act - prohibits unfair monopolies and prescribes the rule of free competition

Facilitated creation of national forest reserves

First US president to be succeeded in office by his predecessor

### Plessy v. Ferguson (1892–1896)

*Historical Events · American · [Notion](https://app.notion.com/p/2d944de2a6568025adf3ce1a7aff43d7)*

50 years before Rosa Parks’s demonstration, Homer Plessy challenged racially segregated train cars. He boarded a “whites-only” section, was arrested, and challenged the reality of the 14th amendment’s “equal protection under the law”

#### Page notes

- Timeline:
  - 1892: Plessy boards a “whites-only” train car and is arrested
  - 1896: Plessy’s case moves to the supreme court
    - Court rules that Louisiana’s segregation law doesn’t violate the 14th amendment as long as the accommodations were equal
    - Ruling rendered racial segregation legal throughout the United States
  - 1954: Overturned by Brown v Board of Education of Topeka
- Context
  - Passage of the 13th amendment post-Civil War didn’t mean equal rights
    - Discriminatory laws after slavery that prevented Black Americans from participating in society on equal ground as whites
  - 1870s: Effort by the government to ensure equal legal status
    - 1876: settlement of presidential election (compromise of 1877)
  - Most of redeemer movement in 1890s; last movement of the federal government for a voting rights act
  - Laws codified existing social practice of segregating housing, schools, public transit
  - Practiced in the North as well as the South, often through segregation, but also through housing and job discrimination
- Agreement between Plessy and the Railroads
  - Railroads didn’t like segregation - a pain to maintain separate cars, a pain for conductors to keep segregation, could be fined by passengers and government for not enforcing it
  - Railroad (Eastern Railway) set up situation with Plessy to challenge the law:
    - Plessy sat in a whites-only car, announces he’s African American, is arrested and fined, Plessy’s lawyers bring claim that the segregation is illegal, brought to Supreme Court
- Harlan’s Dissent - lone dissenter in the Supreme Court
  - Believed in “colorblind Constitution”
    - says with respect to Civil Rights common to all citizens that the Constitution is required to be colorblind
  - Believes right to use transportation is a civil right and is protected by section 1 of 14th amendment
  - BUT voted in favor of segregated schools, former supporter of slavery
  - Note that Supreme Court used to distinguish between civil rights (eg contracts, property, testimony) and social rights (do civil rights in company of people of a different race)
- Effect of the Plessy Ruling
  - State governments allowed to segregate citizens on basis of race
  - Would it have made a difference if the Court had ruled segregation was unconstitutional? Would states have behaved differently?
