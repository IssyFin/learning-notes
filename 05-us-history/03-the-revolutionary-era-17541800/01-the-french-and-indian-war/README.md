### The Seven Years’ War/The French and Indian War (1754–1763)

*Wars · French, English, American · [Notion](https://app.notion.com/p/35444de2a6568003a6e8f2019b48d88a)*

The first “global” war; fought all over the world. Fought between England (and their Native American Allies) and the French (and their Native American allies). Had repercussions including the formation of Canada and the movement of the Acadians (Cajuns) to Louisiana.

#### Page notes

Background and motives

- France and England competing for position as world power
  - Territory in the americas and protection/ resources for colonists
    - Territory in the Caribbean especially - sugar
  - Trade opportunities with Native Americans (fur)
  - Territory in the Ohio river valley ( trade w/ the three rivers, furs, resources)
- Native Americans used opportunity to fight with other Native groups - settle scores, ensure their own alliances, safety, and resources

Major Battles and Events

- Battle at Fort Duquesne - Young George Washington leads charge, horrible loss and leads to construction of fort necessity
- Battle #2 at fort Duquesne: once again, England loses; General Braddock is killed, Washington has to lead the retreat
- Pitt pours money into the cause  at this point, and England comes back from a losing position to gain much more ground. At Battle of Acadia, England forces French Canadian settlers south to Louisiana
- Treaty of Paris (1763): France and England hash out treaty; England effectively gets title of the major world power

Aftermath

- England taxes American colonists to make up for what they lost in war - huge negative sentiment which contributes to the American revolution
- Native Americans treated poorly (now that two powers aren’t vying for their favor) - pushed west,  boundary disrespected by colonists, increase in racial divide

Pontiac’s Rebellion

- In the treaty of Paris, France gave England lots of Native American land, particularly around the great lakes
- England placed more restrictions on native populations - restricted trade and refused to sell ammunition - made alliances and hunting more difficult and generally disrespected native populations
- 1763 - Pontiac and 300 others of the Oshawa tribe attack Fort Detroit
- Though the British successfully defend at first, other native populations join the effort, and they take 8 of 11 posts in the Ohio river valley
- Royal Proclamation of 1763 set a boundary along the Appalachians between white settlers,ent and Native lands. That boundary was not respected by the settlers
