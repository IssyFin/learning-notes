### The Bill of Rights (~1780s)

*Historical Events · American · [Notion](https://app.notion.com/p/36b44de2a656809aa13cfdc01f39660d)*

Ten amendments guaranteeing certain rights and protections by limiting the power of the government

**Sources**

- [Khan Article on the Bill of Rights](https://www.khanacademy.org/humanities/us-history/road-to-revolution/creating-a-nation/a/the-bill-of-rights)

<!-- page-notes:36b44de2a656809aa13cfdc01f39660d -->
#### Page notes

- 1- Freedom of Speech
- 2- Right to Bear Arms
- 3- Restriction from quartering soldiers
- 4- Unreasonable search and seizure
- 5-8 Legal and Criminal proceedings (fair trial, due process, self-incrimination)
- 9- Not an Exhaustive List
- 10- Anything not explicitly delegated to the federal government is left to the states

Marked a sharp break in tradition; clearly expressed the existence of natural rights

### The Constitutional Convention of 1787 (1787)

*Historical Events · American · [Notion](https://app.notion.com/p/36a44de2a656801e9d92ecae8c9beac3)*

**Sources**

- [Khan Article on the Constitutional Convention of 1787](https://www.khanacademy.org/humanities/us-history/road-to-revolution/creating-a-nation/a/the-constitutional-convention-of-1787)
- [Khan Video on the Constitutional Convention of 1787](https://www.khanacademy.org/humanities/us-history/road-to-revolution/creating-a-nation/v/the-constitutional-convention)

<!-- page-notes:36a44de2a656801e9d92ecae8c9beac3 -->
#### Page notes

- Context
  - Delegates met to address the power balance of the national government
  - Debates and compromises for the new governmental framework; revise and replace the Articles of Confederation
  - 55 delegates from 12 states (Rhode Island did not participate)
- Articles of Confederation
  - Federal government could not
    - Collect taxes
    - Control trade between states
    - Settle disputes between states
  - States were in a loose union - akin to the European Union
- Debates
  - How should Congress be organized? (Unicameral or Bicameral legislature?)
  - How should states be represented in Congress?
  - Congress Plans
    - James Madison’s Virginia Plan: 2 house legislature with # of representatives based on population; primarily supported by large states
    - New Jersey Plan: one-house legislature where every state gets one vote; gave each state equal influence
    - Connecticut Compromise:  Two houses; Senate (every state gets equal # of reps) and the House of Representatives (# reps based on population), executive branch led by the president
  - Slavery; how should enslaved people be counted in determining the number of representatives?
    - The Three-Fifths Compromise: Every 5 enslaved people would count as 3 for House Representation and taxes
- Ratification
  - 9 of the 12 states had to formally approve the articles; smaller states ratified first

### The Federalist Papers (1788)

*Historical Events · American · [Notion](https://app.notion.com/p/36b44de2a6568042a864e38fdd2f73af)*

A collection of 85 essays published in 1788 meant to persuade people to accept and ratify the US Constitution (9 of the 13 states had to ratify it) written by John Jay, Alexander Hamilton, and James Madison

**Sources**

- Khan Article on the Federalist Papers

<!-- page-notes:36b44de2a6568042a864e38fdd2f73af -->
#### Page notes

- Most Influential Papers
  - 1- Questioned whether Societies of Men were capable of establishing good government from reflection and choice or if they were forced to rely on accident and force
  - 10-Madison reflects on how to prevent rule by the majority
  - 39 and 51 - Madison seeks to lay a foundation for different powers of government with checks, division of power between federal and states, and division of power between executive, legislative, and judicial
  - 84- Hamilton argues against a Bill of Rights; listing specific rights might discourage the acceptance of the existence of different rights
