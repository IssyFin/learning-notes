### Townshend Acts (~1760s)

*Historical Events · American · [Notion](https://app.notion.com/p/35844de2a65680c79448dfadd4783fb4)*

**Sources**

- [Khan Article on the Townshend Acts](https://www.khanacademy.org/humanities/us-history/road-to-revolution/x71a94f19:growing-discontent/a/the-townshend-acts-ush)

<!-- page-notes:35844de2a65680c79448dfadd4783fb4 -->
#### Page notes

- Historical Context
  - Britain had just wrapped up the 7 Years’ War and needed substantial resources to make up for wartime expenditures
  - Britain initially tried to recoup losses via the Stamp Act, but intense pushback from colonists necessitated a different path
  - While repealing the Stamp Act, Parliament also passed the Declaratory Act, which “asserted the right of Parliament to make laws for the colonies in all cases whatsoever”
- 1767: Townshend Act
  - Chancellor of the Exchequer Charles Townshend introduced new taxes on imported goods, including glass, lead, paint, and tea, and strengthened customs enforcement, prosecuting smugglers
- Opposition
  - Colonists believed that Parliament had no right to tax them since they were not represented in the government. Britain claimed that they had “virtual representation” since politicians looked after the interests of all British subjects.
  - Royal governors restricted assemblies from meeting, due to their formal resistance and pushback.
  - Colonists boycotted British-made goods and organized protests. Strongest resistance in Boston since it was a major trade hub and was where the Customs Board was based.
  - Britain sent troops to Boston to assert order; set stage for Boston Massacre in 1770.
    - *[Image in Notion: A signed nonimportation agreement, 1767. Public domain.](https://app.notion.com/p/35844de2a65680c79448dfadd4783fb4)*
- Committees of Correspondence
  - Massachusetts governor Thomas Hutchinson (appointed by the Crown) tried to build a network of trusted loyalists to support Britain’s tax and trade policies
  - 1772: Samuel Adams calls for creation of a committee of correspondence; share information, educate citizens about rights, organize resistance to British actions. Keep towns informed and united against British rule.
  - By 1774, each colony formed its own committee of correspondence

### The Stamp Act (1765–1766)

*Historical Events · American · [Notion](https://app.notion.com/p/35844de2a6568074a91af5abb9c42331)*

To recoup losses for the 7 years’ war, Britain placed a tax on the colonies, requiring them to only use paper manufacturered in London and use British coin to pay. Kicked off the “no taxation without representation” aspect of the Revolutionary War sentiments

**Sources**

- [Khan Article on the Stamp Act](https://www.khanacademy.org/humanities/us-history/road-to-revolution/x71a94f19:growing-discontent/a/the-stamp-act)

<!-- page-notes:35844de2a6568074a91af5abb9c42331 -->
#### Page notes

- Historical Context
  - 1750s and 1760s: France + Britain in a war for territory and power (7 years’ war); Britain won but expensive - needed to recoup losses through taxing the American colonies
- 1765: The Stamp Act
  - Prime Minister George Grenville
  - Required all newspapers, legal papers, official documents to be printed on stamped paper from London, purchased with British coin
    - Paper more expensive this way, and British coin harder to find than paper money
  - First law meant to raise money from the colonies themselves
- Opposition
  - William Pitt (Grenville’s brother) opposed the Stamp Act; since American colonists don’t have representation in British government, the government has no right to tax them
  - Primary affected groups (eg publishers, news, politicians, legal proponents) were also very politically active - lots of demonstrations, opposition, protests, etc
  - “No taxation without representation”
  - October 1765: colony delegates met to draft formal petitions to King George III and Parliament
  - First time the colonies worked together to challenge British authority
- Response
  - Grenville replaced by Lord Rockingham during this period, who was more empathetic to the colonists
  - 1766: Stamp Act is repealed
