### Manifest Destiny (1830–1880)

*Historical Periods · American · [Notion](https://app.notion.com/p/22444de2a656800f97cef12dff0869ec)*

American sentiment focused on expanding Westward, both as a right and a duty.

**Sources**

#### Page notes

- Origins
  - 1845 term coined by newspaper editor John O’Sullivan
    - God provided the continent for the US to expand, and it’s the “obvious” course of action to do so. “Limitless” resources available for the taking
  - Ideas and Beliefs were long-standing:
    - European colonization
    - Belief in “superiority” of white christians
    - Belief that expansion westward constituted progress and civilization (even  though it brought along violence and destruction)
- Expansion under Polk’s Presidency
  - Polk promised to extend the US from the Atlantic to the Pacific Ocean
  - Mexican-American War (1846-1848): US gained territory of California, Nevada, New Mexico, Arizona, Utah
  - Oregon Treaty of 1846: Polk resolved a border dispute with Great Britain; set US-Canada border at the 49th parallel, giving US territory of Oregon, Washington, Montana, Wyoming, and Idaho
    ![American Progress, by John Gast, 1872](https://cdn.kastatic.org/ka-perseus-images/4b48cf863c565d40a81c9f12ba28ed156955ca08.jpg)
- Causes
  - Immigrants drawn by economic opportunity
    - Gold Rush in California near the Sierra Nevada Mountains - San Francisco was the nearest “gateway” port to that trove, and population exploded from 1848-1850, and even more after that (\~7,000 in 1848 to 300,000 in 1860)
    - As land became scarce in the east, farmers began moving west via the oregon trail
      - Homestead act granted land to anyone willing to go settle and improve it; wartime expansion of the free soil movement
    - 1862: Pacific Railroad Act (granted lots of land for completion of the railroad)
  - Growth of the railroad, and necessary building of communities to support those moving westward for gold
  - Cultural sense of superiority - whites should “civilize” the land
- Consequences
  - Used to justify Indian Removal; Natives were seen as obstacles rather than their own distinct peoples with rights to their homeland
  - Intensified national debate over slavery and regional conflict
    - Northerners and Southerners debated over whether it should be allowed in new Western states
    - Wilmot Proviso (1846) attempted to ban slavery in territory gained from Mexico
  - Intensified racial conflict and violence; against not just Native Americans and Black Americans, but immigrants from other nations (especially Chinese communities)
- Texas joining the Union
  - Historical Context
    - Mexico gained independence from Spain in 1821, and included territory in Texas, California, and New Mexico
    - Since Mexico was sparsely populated, they encouraged US settlers to move there
    - About 7,000 US settlers moved to Mexico, but were pressured by requirements for converting to Catholocism, becoming Mexican citizens, and ending slave labor (Mexico outlawed slavery in 1829)
  - Texas declares independence in 1835:
    - US settlers and Tejanos (Texans of Mexican heritage) in Texas declared independence from Mexico in 1835; Mexico’s president (General Antonio Lopez de Santa Ana) led troops to regain control
      - Mexico leads a 13-day siege on the Alamo; Santa Ana and his 5,000 troops capture the Alamo, killing all defenders (including Davy Crockett, Jim Bowie, William Travis)
        - *[Image in Notion: The Battle of the Alamo (Wikimedia Commons)](https://app.notion.com/p/22444de2a656800f97cef12dff0869ec)*
    - A month after the Battle of the Alamo, Sam Houston led forces to victory at the Battle of San Jacinto, capturing Santa Ana and forcing him to accept Texas independence
  - Texas exists as an independent nation from 1836-1845
    - Many Texans wanted to join US for protection from Mexico (at the time, Mexico didn’t recognize Texan independence) and boost the economy through trade
    - Annexing Texas was a hot topic in the US - since Texas allowed slavery, many Southerners supported its annexation, while many northerners rejected it
    - 1845: Congress votes, and Texas joins the Union under Polk’s presidency
  - The Mexican-American War (1846-1848)
    - Mexican-American War started over contest over the territory; Mexico claimed the Nueces River to be Texas’s southern border, while US claimed it to be the Rio Grande River
    - On US victory, Texas’s southern border solidified at the Rio Grande, and US gained territory in Nevada, Utah, California, New Mexico, and Wyoming

### Mexican-American War (1846–1848)

*Wars · American, Mexican · [Notion](https://app.notion.com/p/22344de2a65680f4a149e854168aa35c)*

![Mexican-American War](https://constitutioncenter.org/images/uploads/blog/Battle_of_Churubusco.jpg)

US vs Mexico

Fight over annexation of Texas and US desire to expand westward. US provoked Mexico to “start” the war in goal for more territory. US gained its current southwest area. Further increased tensions between North and South.

**Sources**

- [Khan Video on the Mexican-American War](https://www.khanacademy.org/humanities/us-history/civil-war-era/x71a94f19:the-mexican-american-war/v/the-mexican-american-war)

#### Page notes

- US push to expand westward
  - 3 fronts: California, New Mexico, Mexico
  - Treaty of Guadalupe Hidalgo (1948) - US gained MExican Cession (all California, Nevada, Utah, most of Arizona, some Colorado, Wyoming, New Mexico
  - Transformative in national politics and everyday life
    - Some people went to sleep in Mexico and woke up in the US; new laws, customs, rights
- Causes
  - April 1846: Mexican cavalry brigade attacked US forces under the command of Zachary Taylor: Mattamoris, across from the Rio Grande
    - Polk sent war message to Congress - American blood was shed on American soil
  - Texas annexation
    - Since the 1820s, American settlers moved into Texas region. Mexico outlawed slavery, but US kept pushing, rebelling against Mexico
    - Texas existed as an independent nation until 1845, since adding another slave state could upset the precarious political balance
  - Manifest Destiny (God wants the US to expand)
    - 1845: James Polk takes office and annexes Texas
      - Polk offers to Mexico to buy California, Mexico refuses
      - Disputed border halfway through Texas:
        - Taylor’s troops were technically invading Mexico’s territory, but b/c disputed, Polk can say they attacked US on US soil
  - Many politicians dispute who “actually” started war; skeptical about US “invasion” claim
- Results of War
  - Costly war
  - Treaty of Guadalupe-Hidalgo
    - US gives \$15m to Mexico
    - Mexico ceded southwest US to US
  - Residents of Territory (Mexicans, Native Americans, Descendents of Spanish colonists)
    - Texas constitution permitted slavery, denied civil rights to non-whites
    - Made little difference to native people controlling parts of the west
  - Led to greater sectional tension between North and South (Texas is to be a slave state?)
    - Wilmot Proviso: David Wilmot introduced House Resolution that prohibited slavery in any territory gained by the conflict - broke down party lines
    - Originally, didn’t pass Senate. But California goldrush made statehood a matter of urgency
      - California statehood upset balance between free and slave states

### The Gold Rush (1848–1855)

*Historical Periods · American · [Notion](https://app.notion.com/p/2fb44de2a65680cf826cd3a762d45a6a)*

A mad flocking of easterners and immigrants to the western US in search of wealth.

**Sources**

- [Khan Article on the Gold Rush](https://www.khanacademy.org/humanities/us-history/the-gilded-age/x71a94f19:building-the-american-west/a/the-gold-rush)

<!-- page-notes:2fb44de2a65680cf826cd3a762d45a6a -->
#### Page notes

- Timeline
  - 1848: James W Marshall discovers gold in the soil at the bottom of the river in Sutters Mill in California, spurring herds of fortune-seekers westward
    - Note that prospectors not only came from the Eastern US, but from Asia, Latin America, Europe, and Australia
    - Gold rush: ‘49-ers (year that the gold rush really kicked off)
  - 1850: California formally joins the Union
- Life as a ‘49-er
  - Backbreaking work, and rarely resulted in fortune
    - *[Image in Notion: A forty-niner panning for gold in the American River, 1850 (Wikimedia Commons)](https://app.notion.com/p/2fb44de2a65680cf826cd3a762d45a6a)*
  - Poor living conditions
    - Lack of housing, sanitation, law enforcement
  - Extremely high crime rates → reliance on vigilante justice
- Extreme prejudice of white americans to other groups
  - Chinese and Latin-American immigrants were frequently attacked
  - Establishment of taxes and laws that targeted immigrants (particularly Chinese immigrants)
  - *[Image in Notion: Illustration depicting Chinese gold prospectors during the Gold Rush (Wikimedia Commons)](https://app.notion.com/p/2fb44de2a65680cf826cd3a762d45a6a)*
  - State of California paid bounties for Native American scalps, and politicians called for eradication of Native Americans
    - Vigilante groups of miners, settlers, loggers went after native populations, almost completely eliminating the populations by 1890
