### Emancipation Proclamation (1862)

**Sources**

#### Page notes

- Context
  - Civil War in progress for a year, not going well for Lincoln
  - 11 of 15 Southern states where slavery was legal formed the Confederate States of America
  - Originally, assumed the Union would suppress the rebellion after a few weeks, but Confederates won most of the major battles
- Lincoln’s Dilemma
  - Can’t bring South back to the US by protecting slavery
  - Radical politicians in Lincoln’s party (republicans) saw secession as best opportunity to abolish slavery
  - Northerners pushed Lincoln to punish the states that seceded by making abolition a major goal of the war
    - However, 4 slave states had still not seceded from teh US (Missouri, Kentucky, Maryland, and Delaware) - feared that doing so would push them to the Confederacy
      - *[Image in Notion: The United States in 1862. The states in light blue were "border states," on the border of the North (dark blue) and the South (red). Border states allowed slavery but did not secede along with the rest of the slave states.](https://app.notion.com/p/29144de2a6568062ab3ed67bcd33856b)*
- Towards Emancipation
  - Enslaved people in the South interpreted the conflict as a war to end slavery
    - When Northern forces invaded the South, Black men and women escaped from bondage and ran to US army lines
    - Army unsure what to do with influx of enslaved people
      - Extra mouths for North to feed, *but* demoralized white Southerners and deprived them of their labor force
  - By mid-1862, Lincoln realizes that slavery is a major war issue - committing the US to abolishing slavery would only help its cause
- The Emancipation Proclamation
  - Lincoln presented the draft to his cabinet on July 22, 1862, asking for their opinion
    - Secretary of war Edwin Stanton suggested they wait for a big military victory to issue so it weouldn’t seem like a desperate measure
    - No major victory until September 17, 1862, at the Battle of Antietam
  - Not intended to be touching or poignant, but an iron-clad legal document; decree written, signed, and distributed
  - Slaves would only be freed in states which were “then in rebellion against the United States”
    - Gave those states the opportunity to rejoin the union before Jan 1, 1863 to prevent the proclamation from going into effect (they declined)
    - Did not apply to enslaved people in the border states which had not joined the confederacy
  - Later codified in the 13th amendment
  - Made the US army a true force of liberation
