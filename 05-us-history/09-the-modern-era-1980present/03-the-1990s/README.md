## Policy Debates

### Free Trade and Deregulation

- Republicans: Lower trade restrictions and tariffs on imported goods
- Clinton: Reduce Trade barriers
  - US-Canada Free Trade Agreement (1988) - foundation for North American Free Trade Agreement (NAFTA)
    - Replaced in 2020 by Trump's United States-Mexico-Canada Agreement (USMCA) - to protect American jobs, raise wages for Mexican workers, and strengthen labor and environmental protections
  - Opposition - blue collar workers in the Rust Belt - loss of industrial jobs
### The Social Safety Net

- Social Welfare Programs under attack
  - Introduced by Lyndon B Johnson's Great Society in the 1960s
  - Medicare, Medicaid, Food Assistance programs
  - Reagan cut spending on social programs
  - Clinton limited government assistance during welfare reform - stricter work requirements, shifted responsibility to state government
 
### Financial Regulation

- Banks merged in the 1980s and 1990s, resulting in bigger banks and less competition
  - Repeal of the Glass-Steagall Act in 1999: banks could offer more complex investment products (mortgage backed securities and credit defaults waps)
  - Sparked the Great recession
- 2010 Dodd-Frank Act - restricted banks from making high-risk investments
  - Safeguards necessary to reduce chances of another crisis
- 2018 Economic Growth, Regulatory Relief, and Consumer Protection Act rolled back Dodd-Frank regulations

## The Gulf War (1990–1991)

![Gulf War](https://negativecolors.com/wp-content/uploads/2020/08/Image32a.jpg)

### Context

- Started August 2, 1990 - forces of Iraqi dictator Saddam Hussein invaded oil-rich Kuwait
  - Kuwait oil reserves would help pay off massive debt in the Iraq-Iran war
  - Give Iraq bargaining power as the gatekeeper to Middle Eastern oil
- US concerns:
  - Invasion of Kuwait a first-step by Iraq into becoming a major power in the Middle East (eg over Saudi Arabia)
  - Oil crisis if Hussein controlled Kuwait and Saudi Arabia (over 20% of the world's oil reserves)
- Iraq - world's fourth largest military force (partially because US furnished weapons to Iraq in its fight against Iran)
  - Note on US-Iran relations: 1979, Islamic fundamentalists ousted pro-American government in Iran and took American embassy workers hostage
- War in Iran (/Kuwait) only lasted until February 1991, but war started with Iraq again in 2003 following discovery of Hussein's possession of WMDs and the terrorist attacks of 9/11
 
### Initial Reactions

- US and UN Security Council condemned the invasion - placed economic sanctions on Iraq with deadline of 1991 to withdraw troops from Kuwait

### Stage 1: Operation Desert Shield (August 1990 - January 1991)

- Bush built coalition of 34 nations in the UN, increased US troop strength in region to over 500,000
- "Line in the sand" to protect Saudi Arabia and liberate Kuwait
- The combat stage of the conflict - 5-week bombing campaign
- Led by General Colin Powell and General Norman Schwarzkopf
- Iran responded with short-ranged "Scud" missiles at civilian and military targets in Saudi Arabia and Israel
- First American Conflict shown on live television (Vietnam War coverage lagged several days) - CNN aired live images of bombs exploding


### Stage 2: Operation Desert Storm (January 1991 - February 1991)

- Iraqi forces fled Kuwait on February 28, setting fire to hundreds of oil wells
- Bush declared ceasefire and Kuwait was liberated
- Hussein was allowed to remain in power


## The Presidency of Bill Clinton (1993–2001)

*Historical Figures · American · [Notion](https://app.notion.com/p/23e44de2a65680f298eecf581dbc45b5)*

![Bill Clinton](https://upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/d/d3/Bill_Clinton.jpg/500px-Bill_Clinton.jpg)

US President (Democrat)

### Overview

- Considered a centrist, “Third Way” political philosophy; New Democrat
- Presided over the second longest period of peacetime economic expansion.
- Signed NAFTA into law and Violent Crime Control and Law Enforcement Act. Failed to plass pan for healthcare reform.
- Became conservative in domestic policy, signing: Personal Responsibility and Work Opportunity Act, State Children’s Health Insurance Program, and financial deregulation measures. Appointed RBG and Stephen Breyer to the Supreme Court.
- Ordered US military intervention in the Bosnian and Kosovo wars, signed Dayton Peace Agreement.
- Called for expansion of NATO in Eastern Europe.
- Signed Iraq Liberation Act which gave aid to groups against Saddam Hussein. Participated in Oslo I Accord and Camp David Summit to advance the Israeli-Palestinian peace process, assisted Northern Ireland peace process.
- Second term tainted with Clinton-Lewinsky scandal. FIrst president to be impeached since Andrew Johnson - impeached on perjury and powers of the presidency to commit obstruction of justice. Aquitted on both charges.

- Fun facts:
  - youngest president to serve 2 full terms
  - First president to be born in the Baby Boomer generation
  - Last 3 years of presidency, the Congressional Budget office reported the first and only budget surplus since 1969.
  - Left office with the joint-highest approval rating of any US president.

### Presidential Race

- 3-way Race against George HW Bush and Ross Perot (independent)
- Centrist wing - supported free markets, trade agreements, and reducing federal budget deficit

### Deficit Reduction and Economic Growth

- 1993 - Omnibus Budget Reconciliation Act - raised taxes on high-income earners, reduce federal spending
- Economy grew following the 1993 Act; rise of technology companies and expansion of the internet - *first federal budget surplus in decades*
- 1994 - North American Free Trade Agreement (NAFTA) - reduced trade barriers between US, Canada, Mexico
  - Resulted in improvement of economy but hurt manufacturing regions
 
### Divided Government

- 1994 - Republicans won control of Congress (*first time in 40 years*)
- Contract with America - led by Speaker of the House Newt Gingrich - reduce government spending, cut taxes, reform welfare
- Clashes between President Clinton and Republican-controlled congress: 2 federal government shutdowns in 1995 and 1996 over budget
- 1996 - Personal Responsibility and Work Opportunity Reconciliation Act
  - Added work requirements and time limits to welfare benefits
  - Shifted responsibility to state governments
 
### Scandal and Impeachment

- 1998 - HoR impeached Clinton on charges of perjury and obstruction of justice after he denied having an affair with White House intern Monica Lewinsky
- Senate held trial but didn't reach the 2/3 vote needed to remove him from office

## Globalization since the 1990s

**Globalization**: The growing connections between countries and people all over the world, involving:
- Trade in goods and services
- International investment
- Cultural Exchange
- Communication through digital technology
- Migration across borders

### Globalization Acceleration in the 1990s

- Causes
  - End of Cold War: increased international trade
  - Technological advances, including internet, mobile phones, satellite communication
  - Free trade agreements
  - Integration of previously economically isolated countries into global markets
  - Lowered trade barriers and increased investment
- Economic Effects
  - Cheaper goods for consumers
  - New markets for companies
  - Job losses across some industries (including manufacturing in US and Europe)
  - Companies moving manufacturing overseas (lwoer labor costs)
- Risks of Global Interdependence
  - Great Recession of 2008-2009 in US became a global crisis
  - Banks and markets affected by deeply interconnected financial systems
  - COVID-19 showed similar supply chain disruptions - how much to rely on foreign manufacturing?
  - Global terrorist networks
  - Weaken local traditions or concentrate cultural influence in powerful countries
 
It seems that, in the 2020s, globalization may be slowing down - countries are reshoring closer to home. 


## The Election of 2000

### Candidates

- Republican George W Bush (governor of Texas and son of George HW Bush)
  - Vs John McCain in the Primaries
  - Chose Secretary of Defense Dick Cheney as his running mate
- Democrat Al Gore (senator from Tennessee and VP of Bill Clinton)
- Focused campaign on domestic issues (economic growth, federal budget surplus, health care, tax relief, and selfare programs)

### Results

- Gore won the popular vote by over half a million votes, but lacked the electoral votes (255 Gore vs 246 Bush, remaining belonging to Florida)
- News Stations declared the call too early, and later withdrew - election too close to call the following morning
- Automatic machine recount reduced Bush's lead to just a few hundred votes
  - Hanging Chads - punchcard ballots that couldn't be properly read by machines
  - Butterfly ballots which may have confused voters
  - Gore campaign requested hand recounts in heavily-Democratic counties
  - Bush campaign appealed to Supreme Court
- Dispute not resolved until just weeks before the presidential inauguration
- Led to changes in voting systems across the country
