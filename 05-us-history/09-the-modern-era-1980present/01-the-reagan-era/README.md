## Summary

The 1980s was a battle between ideologies - a push towards conservative economics and trickledown (through Reaganomics), a wrapping up of the Cold War, and discrimination and activism associated with the AIDS epidemic.

## The Presidency of Ronald Reagan

### Ronald Reagan (1981–1989) Overview

*Historical Figures · American · [Notion](https://app.notion.com/p/23a44de2a65680c694e7c1baf74834d7)*

![Ronald Reagan](https://upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/1/16/Official_Portrait_of_President_Reagan_1981.jpg/250px-Official_Portrait_of_President_Reagan_1981.jpg)

US President (Republican)
Led one of the strongest conservative political movements in American history.

### Background

- Before entering politics, worked as a Hollywood actor, president of Screen Actors Guild (Union for Actors) and spokesperson for GE
- Spent most of his life as a liberal Democrat until his views changed in the 1950s, officially switched parties in 1962
- Served as governor of California from 1967-1975; criticized growth of government programs and bigger government roles
- Campaign focused on his economic plan and returning to spiritual values
  - Cut taxes
  - Increase military spending
  - Promote deregulation
  - Balance the federal budget
  - Return to spiritual values
  - End double-digit inflation characterizing Jimmy Carter's presidency
- "Let's make America great again"


### Reaganomics

- Economic plan known as supply-side economics - grow the economy by cutting taxes and reducing government rules on business - "trickle down"
  - 25% cut in income taxes, spread out over 3 stages
  - Tax Reform Act of 1986: Lowered top marginal tax rate (rate paid by highest earners) to 28%
  - Deregulation: Trucking and telephone industries deregulated and clean air standards for cars was lowered
- Tried to reduce government spending (particularly on major social welfare problems) but many actions stopped by Congress
  - Cuts still made to food stamps, low-income housing, and school lunch programs, lowered federal funding for education

### Key Actions 

- Implemented “Reaganomics”, involving economic deregulation and cuts in taxes and government spending during a period of stagflation. (That said, National Debt nearly tripled since 1981 as a result of tax cuts and increased military spending
- Escalated arms race, increased military spending, transitioned Cold War policy away from policies of detente with Soviet Union (effectively re-straining relations)
- Ordered 1983 invasion of Grenada
- Slow to respond to the AIDS epidemic, fought public-sector labor unions, expanded war on drugs
- 1986 bombing of Libya, Iran-Contra affair, negotiations with Soviet Leader culminated in Intermediate-Range Nuclear Forces Treaty

## Foreign Policy Under Reagan

### Confronting the Soviet Union

- Framed Soviet Union as an "evil empire" with the Cold War as a moral struggle between freedom and totalitarianism
- Military actions
  - Massive military expansion
  - Strategic Defense Initiative (SDI) - proposed space-based system to destroy incoming Soviet missiles (debatable whether it would work)
  - 1983: deployed nuclear missiles in several NATO countries to counter Soviet weapons in Europe
- Rollback: Actively trying to reduce Soviet influence, rather than just containment. Provided support to:
  - Mujahideen fighting Soviet forces in Afghanistan
  - Contras, who opposed Nicaragua's Marxist government
  - Anti-communist rebels in Angola
- Invaded Grenada in 1983
  - Prompted after coup resulted in Marxists in power
  - US replaced the government
- Still supported other authoritarian governments which opposed leftist movements
  - El Salvador
  - Guatemala
- After the Cold War, some changes in Soviet Union Government; Reagan and Gorbachev worked together
  - Soviet Reforms
    - glasnost - greater openness
    - perestroika - economic restructuring
  - Intermediate-Range Nuclear Forces (INF) Treaty in 1987: first agreement to eliminate an entire category of nuclear weapons
  - 1987: Reagan challenges Soviet Union on the Berlin Wall "Mr. Gorbachev, tear down this wall"
 
### Middle East and North Africa

- Context: Instability in Middle East and North Africa
  - 1981 - Day Reagan takes office, Iran releases last of American hostages (held for 444 days)
  - 1983 - suicide bombings in Beirut killed 17 Americans at the US Embassy. Reagan withdrew forces shortly thereafter
  - 1986 - Evidence found linking Libya to a Berlin bombing which killed American soldiers, and Reagan orders airstrikes
 
### The Iran-Contra Affair

- Overview
  - Boland Agreement - Congress banned US funding for the Contras in Nicaragua
  - Reagan administration members secretly sold weapons to Iran to improve relations, used profits to fund contras
  - Operation became public in 1986, scandalizing the administration
- 1986 - US supply plain is shot down over Nicaragua, surviving crew member revealed what was going on; US attorney general confirmed that $70M from Iran arms sales were diverted to the contras
- Reagan admitted that he approved the weapons sales, but no evidence could link him to the knowledge of that money going to the contras - the "Teflon President" 

## The AIDS Epidemic of the 1980s

### The Emergence of AIDS

- Timeline
  - 1981 - CDC reports unusual causes of a rare cancer and severe infections in cities (LA, San Francisco, NY) with death within months
  - 1982 - Disease officially named acquired immunodeficiency syndrome (AIDS)
  - 1983 - CDC confirmed AIDS could not spread through casual contact
  - 1984 -  HIV identified as the virus that causes AIDS
  - 1987 - FDA approves AZT (first drug shown to slow progression of HIV)
  - Mid-1990s - Combinations of medications (antiretroviral therapy) hugely improved survival rates
  - Today - Treatments so successful that people living with HIV can have viruses at undetectable and untransmittable levels
- Activism and Community Response
  - LGBTQ+ community organized clinics, support groups, and provided education
  - Activists pushed federal government to act - funding for research, treatment, and public education
  - Staged protests
  - AIDS memorial quilt - first displayed in Washington, DC - each panel honored someone who died from AIDS to show the human cost of the epidemic
- Discrimination
  - 1980s full of discrimination against those thought to be infected, due to fear and a sense of superiority ("punishment" for "immoral" behavior)
  - 1985 - Ryan White (13-year old who contracted HIV through a blood transfusion) barred from attending school, helped to change public attitudes
  - Public figures announcing they were struggling through the disease (Actor Rock Hudson, Magic Johnson) challenged stereotypes and reduced stigma
- Government Response was slow - Reagan didn't publicly address AIDS until several years after it became a major health emergency
