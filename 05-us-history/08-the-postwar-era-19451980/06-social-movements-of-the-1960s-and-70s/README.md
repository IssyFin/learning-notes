### Second-Wave Feminism (1960)

*Historical Periods · American · [Notion](https://app.notion.com/p/25a44de2a6568078b43aee9987b22f61)*

Inspired by the Civil Rights Movement, women of all ages began a fight to secure a stronger role in American Society. Resulted in Title VII (section of the Civil Rights Act of 1964 prohibiting discrimination in employment based on gender)

<!-- page-notes:25a44de2a6568078b43aee9987b22f61 -->
#### Page notes

<details>
<summary>Context</summary>

- 1950s and 1960s- Civil Rights Movement created a climate of protests and activism for claiming rights and positions in society for marginalized groups
- Women filled roles in organizations fighting for civil rights, including the **Student National Coordinating Committee (SNCC)** and **Students for a Democratic Society (SDS)**. Those organizations were still influenced by patriarchal ideas of male superiority
  - Casey Hayden and Mary King of the SNCC presented their concerns about the organization’s treatment of women in their document “On the Position of Women in SNCC” which argued that SNCC discriminated against women, similarly to how whites discriminated against African Americans.
    - Stokely Carmichael (future chairman of the SNCC) joked that the position for women in the movement was “prone”
- Civil Rights Act of 1964 (which prohibited discrimination based on race, color, national origin, and religion) also prohibited discrimination on the basis of sex via Title VII
  - Ironically, Title VII was originally suggested in an attempt to *prevent* the act’s passage, the proposer believing the idea was too radical for any of his male colleagues to consider

</details>

<details>
<summary>The Feminine Mystique and NOW</summary>

- First wave feminism considered to be the first women’s rights movement of the nineteenth century
- Second-wave feminism created by white middle-class women, many of whom were older, married women who felt unfulfilled by the roles of housewife and mother
- <u>**The Feminine Mystique**</u> (1963, Betty Friedan)
  - Nonfiction book in which Friedman contested post-WWII belief that it was woman’s destiny to marry and bear children
  - Raised consciousness of many women who agreed that homemaking in the suburbs took their individualism and left them unsatisfied
- **NOW: National Organization for Women**
  - Set an agenda for the feminist movement; statement of purpose written by Friedan
    - Goal to make possible women’s participation in all aspects of American life, gain equal rights enjoyed by men
  - **Equal Rights Amendment (ERA)**
    - Proposed Constitutional Amendment guaranteeing equal rights for women
    - Introduced in Congress in 1923, passed in 1972, but failed to receive the 38 state ratifications necessary to become part of the constitution

</details>

<details>
<summary>The Pill</summary>

- 1960- FDA approved the birth control pill, freeing women from restrictions of pregnancy and childbearing
  - Women could limit, delay, and prevent reproduction and be free to work, attend college, and delay marriage
- First medicine ever intended to be taken by people who were not sick
- Even conservatives considered it a way to make marriages stronger by removing fear of unwanted pregnancy and improving health of women
- Opponents feared it promoted sexual promiscuity and undermine institutions of marriage, family, and moral code
- By 1960s, 30 states made it a criminal offense to sell contraceptive devices

</details>

<details>
<summary>Radical Feminism</summary>

- More radical feminists didn’t want the movement to stop at economic issues; created consciousness-raising events and symbolic attacks on women’s oppression
  - September 1968 demonstration by New York Radical Women: demonstrated at Miss America Pageant in Atlantic City, NJ; crowned a sheep Miss America and tossed high heels, curlers, girdles, and bras into a freedom trash can - described as a bra burning

</details>

### The Delano Grape Strike (1965–1970)

*Historical Events · American · [Notion](https://app.notion.com/p/25a44de2a65680caadacde922458a25c)*

The second longest strike in history, led by migrant workers, which set the precedent for agricultural labor relations in California

**Sources**

- [Khan Outline](https://www.khanacademy.org/humanities/us-history/postwarera/1960s-america/a/the-delano-grape-strike)

<!-- page-notes:25a44de2a65680caadacde922458a25c -->
#### Page notes

<details>
<summary>Context</summary>

- Delano: A central California agricultural town, primary crop is grapes
  - August-October, hire migrant workers to harvest grapes
- Workers secured strike demands, but when workers arrived in Delano, faced resistance from the growers
  - workers demands: hourly wages raised from 1.25 to 1.40, 25 cents from each box of grapes sold, a raise from the 10 cents they typically received
- Since demands weren’t actually met by growers, over 800 Filipino workers voted to strike 10 vineyards

</details>

<details>
<summary>Significance of the strike</summary>

- Difficult decision to make; many workers lived in company housing and had to leave due to the strike
  - Also lost wages for months
- Nearly 3,000 workers committed
- Unions first pursued a boycott, finding allies in the middlemen who sold the products
  - Grapes were perishable, so immediate effect from the boycotts
  - With harvest season ending, unions needed to adapt their strategy since fewer grape products available for sale
  - Organized picket lines in front of grocery stores, asking shoppers not to buy grapes; opposition continued in other areas where grapes were exported, including Canada, Sweden, Finland, Norway, Denmark, and the Netherlands
- NFWA led a 300-mile march, starting in Delano and ending in Sacramento
- In April 1966, Schenley Industries (one of the largest grape producers in California) agreed to provide better wages and working conditions
- By 1970, the other growers gave up; 26 gathered to sign collective bargaining agreements
- Other strikes to follow, emulating the Delano Grape Strike
  - Salad Bowls Strikes in 1970
  - Lettuce Strike of 1973
  - Led to the passage of the California Agricultural Labor Relations Act of 1975
    - Secured farmworkers’ right to form and join labor organizations, define unfair labor practices, and established the California Agricultural Labor Relations Board

</details>

### Liberation Movements of the 1970s (1970–1979)

*Historical Periods · American · [Notion](https://app.notion.com/p/26144de2a65680d79e28c0b7d1f38e63)*

Native Americans, gay men, lesbians, and women organized to change discriminatory laws, strategy known as identity politics (political movements or actions intended to further the interests of a particular group based on culture, race, ethnicity, religion, sex, gender, or sexual orientation

**Sources**

- [Khan Outline on Liberation Movements of the 1970s](https://www.khanacademy.org/humanities/us-history/postwarera/1970s-america/a/liberation-movements-1970s)

<!-- page-notes:26144de2a65680d79e28c0b7d1f38e63 -->
#### Page notes

<details>
<summary>Context</summary>

- Many started to lose hope that society could reunite with common interests and goals
- Focus shifted to issues of significance of the subgroups they belonged to

</details>

<details>
<summary>Native American Protest</summary>

- Many Native Americans sought to maintain their culture or retrieve lost cultural elements
- American Indian Movement (AIM): Urban dwellers frustrated by poverty and discrimination
  - 1970: Average life expectancy for a Native American person was 46 years, vs the national average of 69
  - Half of Native Americans lived on reservations, where unemployment reached 50%
- 1969: Small group of Native American activists planned to build a Native American cultural center on Alcatraz Island, with a history museum, an ecology center, and a spiritual sanctuary
  - Government forces removed final holdouts on June 11, 1971 (19 months after occupation started)
- 1972: AIM marches on Washington, DC: the Trail of Broken Treaties
  - Occupied the offices of the Bureau of Indian Affairs
  - Presented list of demands (improved housing, education, and economic opportunities, drafting of new treaties, return of Native American lands, and protections for native religions and culture)
- 1973: Occupation of Wounded Knee (on Pine Ridge Indian Reservation)
  - Site was historically an 1890 massacre of members of the Lakota tribe by the US army, siege of 71 days with frequent gunfire from both sides.
- Protesters’ demands generally not met, but some land restored, and increased funding for education, healthcare, legal services, housing, and economic development

</details>

<details>
<summary>Gay Rights</summary>

- Mattachine Society: First postwar organization for gay civil rights, Los Angeles in 1950
- Daughters of Bilitis: First national organization for lesbians, in 1955
- San Francisco, in 1966, became home to the National Transsexual Counseling Unit (world’s first organization for transgender people)
  - note- transsexual is an older term used by doctors and psychologists to describe transgender people
- 1967- Sexual Freedom League of San Francisco
- Stonewall Inn: 1969, police raided a Greenwich Village gay bar
  - These raids were common, but the response to this raid was groundbreaking
  - Police started to arrest many of the customers (especially transgender people and cross-dressers)
  - Crowd attacked, angered by the brutal treatment of the prisoners
  - Beer bottles and bricks were thrown, police barricaded themselves inside, and the riot resumed the following night
  - Resulted in the formation of the Gay Liberation Front and Gay Activists’ Alliance
    - Protested discrimination, homophobia, and violence against gay people, and promoted gay liberation and gay pride

</details>

<details>
<summary>Women’s Liberation</summary>

- Feminists opened battered women’s shelters and fought for protections:
  - Employment discrimination for pregnant women
  - Reform of rape laws (abolition of laws requiring a witness to corroborate a woman’s report of rape)
  - Criminalization of domestic violence
  - Funding for schools that sought to counter sexist stereotypes of women
- 1973: Roe v Wade
  - US Supreme Court affirmed state laws under which abortions obtained during the first three months of pregnancy were legal
  - Nontherapeutic abortion then considered a legal medical procedure nationwide
- Education Amendments Act of 1972, Title IX
  - Prohibits sex discrimination in education
- National Organization for Women (NOW) pushed the Equal Rights Amendment
  - Failed to receive enough states votes for ratification and never passed, due to the opposition of socially conservative grassroots organizations

</details>
