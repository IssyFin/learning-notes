### Richard Nixon (1969–1974)

*Historical Figures · American · [Notion](https://app.notion.com/p/23a44de2a656804db868dd1e6101133c)*

![Richard Nixon](https://upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/2/2c/Richard_Nixon_presidential_portrait_%281%29.jpg/250px-Richard_Nixon_presidential_portrait_%281%29.jpg)

US President (Republican)

Campaign focused on the “silent majority” (middle-class white Americans) and employed the “Southern Strategy” to appeal to Southern racists resentful of civil rights activism and federal antipoverty programs

Saw reduction of US involvement in the Vietnam War, relaxing strained relations with Soviet Union and China, Apollo 11 Moon landing, and establishment of OSHA and the EPA. Passed Endangered Species and Clean Air Acts. Implemented Twenty Sixth Amendment to lower voting age from 21 to 18; ended direct international convertability of the USD to gold, taking the US off the gold standard. Imposed wage and price controls for 90 days, launched Wars on Cancer and Drugs, passed the Controlled Substances Act.

Ordered military operations and carpet bombing campaigns in Cambodia (to bring North Vietnamese to the negotiating table)

Aided Pakistan during the Bangladesh Liberation War in 1971

Ended American combat involvement in Vietnam and the military draft in the same year

Finalized Anti-Ballistic Missile Treaty with Soviet Union

Ordered airlift to resupply Israeli material losses in the Yom Kippur War; led to the oil crisis at home.

Fun facts:  
Only president to resign from office (result of the Watergate scandal)

#### Page notes

<details>
<summary>1968 Election</summary>

**Tumultuous Context**

- Year witnessed the assassinations of MLK Jr and RFK (younger brother of JFK campaigning for presidential nomination), and splintering of the Democratic Party
- General fear that the nation’s social fabric was ripping apart)
- Democratic Party Disarray
  - 1968 Democratic National Convention in Chicago demonstrated division of the Democratic party, with chaos and law enforcement action in the form of clubbings with nightsticks and teargas
  - New Left: students and members of the counterculture
  - Old Left/New Deal: older generation of Democrats

**Campaign**

- Nixon’s campaign sought to appeal to the “silent majority”, the middle-class white Americans who defended status quo against radical Social Change
- Nixon pledged to return nation to “law and order”, aka intention to crack down on protestors, activists, and anyone challenging status quo
- Southern Strategy: Appeal to Southern racists resentful of civil rights activism and federal antipoverty programs
- Close race - Nixon garnered 43.4% of the popular vote vs Humphrey’s 42.7%. Impressively, independent candidate George Wallace secured 13.5% of popular vote
- Following Nixon, Republicans would dominate the White House until election of Bill Clinton in 1992

</details>

<details>
<summary>Policies</summary>

**Vietnam**

- Promised a “secret plan” to get the US out of Vietnam
  - First: US undertake Vietnamization (slowly replace American soldiers with South Vietnamese soldiers)
  - Second: Carry the war into neighboring Cambodia (officially neutral but conduit for North Vietnamese and Viet Cong troops)
    - Resulted in wave of protests. National Guard put down riots at Kent State University, shooting and killing 4 young people and wounding 9 others
  - Brought morale of the remaining soldiers to the lowest point yet (reason for fighting uncertain)
- Any response to Vietnam War that wasn’t immediate end was generally criticized by Americans

**Foreign Policy**

- Reopened diplomatic relationship with the People’s Republic of China (previously refused to recognize since communist revolution in 1949
- Nixon and USSR agreed to détente (relaxed tensions) and signed Strategic Arms Limitation Treaty (SALT) to reduce the number of nuclear missiles in the arsenal

**Domestic Policy**

- Campaigned to counteract liberal rulings of the 1950s and 1960s
- Failed to cope with growing issue of stagflation (combination of wage stagnation and price inflation)
  - Announced 90 day wage and price freeze
  - To increase American exports, took the dollar off the gold standard

</details>

<details>
<summary>Watergate</summary>

- Nixon believed everyone was plotting against him
- Nixon loyalists wiretapped the DNC office in Washington’s Watergate building to spy on George McGovern (Democratic nominee)
- Tapes of Nixon’s conversations in the Oval Office revealed that h forbid the FBI from investigating the incident
  - Refused to turn over the tapes of his conversations; House Judiciary Committee recommended that the House of Representatives impeach him for obstruction of justice and abuse of power
- Nixon was pardoned by Vice President Gerald Ford

</details>

<details>
<summary>Watergate Context</summary>

- Nixon played lots of political games throughout his career, made an art of employing “dirty tricks” to win elections, and made many enemies before making his way into the whitehouse
- Following the leak of the Pentagon Papers, Nixon became obsessed with maintaining secrecy in his administration, even employing a group of aides that he called “plumbers” to prevent other leaks
- Nixon’s fundraising organisation: Committee to Reelect the President (CREEP) conducted illegal activities to maintain president’s power and harass individuals on internally-circulated “enemy list”.
  - Forged documents that might incriminate or embarrass Democratic opponents
  - Conducted illegal surveillance
  - Broke into psychiatrist’s office to steal information to discredit a political enemy
  - Placed spies undercover in Democratic campaigns and press corps
  - Rented facilities and ordered campaign supplies in name of Democratic challengers and stuck them with the bill
  - CREEP broke into DC’s Watergate office, and was revealed to have ties with the CIA.
- No hard evidence connecting Nixon to their wrongdoings, but Nixon refused to cooperate
  - Tapes were subpoenaed
  - “Smoking gun” tape where Nixon ordered the FBI not to investigate the break-in any further
    - Smoking gun: trope in mystery and crime stories proving that an individual committed a crime (eg holding a smoking gun after a shot has been fired)
- Nixon’s vice president Spiro Agnew was forced to resign after charged with taking bribes; Gerald Ford was then appointed as his successor

</details>

### The Oil Crisis and Stagflation (1971–1979)

*Historical Events · American · [Notion](https://app.notion.com/p/26144de2a6568089b9fafd292a469c57)*

Economic downturn due to decline of manufacturing jobs, increased competition, and the expense of the Vietnam War. Nixon tried to address by devaluing the dollar and declaring wage- and price-freezes.

Oil-rich nations in the Middle East declared an embargo against the US to retaliate for its support of Israel.

**Sources**

- [Khan Outline on Stagflation and the Oil Crisis](https://www.khanacademy.org/humanities/us-history/postwarera/1970s-america/a/stagflation-and-the-oil-crisis)

<!-- page-notes:26144de2a6568089b9fafd292a469c57 -->
#### Page notes

<details>
<summary>Context</summary>

- Though the 25 years following WWII were economically prosperous for the US, the economy downturned in the ‘70s due to the Vietnam War, the expansion of social programs without tax increases, and reduction in manufacturing

</details>

<details>
<summary>US Response</summary>

- 1971: Nixon imposed a 90-day wage and price freeze and took the dollar off the gold standard to devalue currency
  - Short-term improvement (long enough to get Nixon reelected in 1972) but didn’t address the root issue
- 1973: US supported Israel after a surprise attack by Egypt and Syria in the Yom Kippur War
  - Oil-rich nations responded by announcing an oil shipping embargo against the US as well as Israel’s European allies
    - Already angry with the US for devaluing the dollar (currency used to purchase oil)
    - Led by Saudi Arabia: Organization of the Petroleum Exporting Countries (OPEC)
  - Dramatic increase in price of oil

</details>

### Gerald Ford (1974–1977)

*Historical Figures · American · [Notion](https://app.notion.com/p/23a44de2a65680578c0acdc835bb1b02)*

![Gerald Ford](https://upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/3/36/Gerald_Ford_presidential_portrait_%28cropped%29.jpg/250px-Gerald_Ford_presidential_portrait_%28cropped%29.jpg)

US President (Republican)

Assumed office following Nixon’s resignation (also assumed vice presidency following Spiro Agnew’s resignation)

Presided over the worst economy in the four decades since the Great Depression, with growing inflation and a recession. Granted presidential pardon to Nixon for Watergate

Foreign policy characterized by the increased role of Congress and the corresponding curb on the powers of the president.

Signed the Helsinki Accords, which moved towards eased relations in the Cold War. Collapse of South Vietnam nine months into the presidency.

Fun facts:  
The only person to serve as president without winning an election for president or vice president

### Jimmy Carter (1977–1981)

*Historical Figures · American · [Notion](https://app.notion.com/p/23a44de2a65680298781df2b8a74246f)*

![Jimmy Carter](https://upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/5/5a/JimmyCarterPortrait2.jpg/250px-JimmyCarterPortrait2.jpg)

US President (Democratic)

Pardoned all Vietnam draft invaders and negotiated major foreign policy agreements: Camp David Accords (Political Agreements between Egypt and Israel), Panama Canal Treaties (Guaranteed Panama control of the canal after 1999), Second round of Strategic Arms Limitation Talks (Treaties between the US and Soviet Union), established diplomatic relations with China.

Created a national energy policy including conservation, price control, and new technology.

Signed bills to create the Departments of Energy and Education

Fun facts: A major contributor to the eradication of the guinea worm

**Sources**

- [Khan Outline on the Presidency of Jimmy Carter](https://www.khanacademy.org/humanities/us-history/postwarera/1970s-america/a/the-presidency-of-jimmy-carter)

#### Page notes

<details>
<summary>The Election of Jimmy Carter</summary>

- US in bad shape; economy poor and Watergate scandal eroded trust in elected officials
- Jimmy Carter campaigned as an outsider, a pious and forthright man who wouldn’t lie to the voters

</details>

<details>
<summary>The Presidency of Jimmy Carter</summary>

- Alienation
  - Unwilling to work with Washington to achieve results
  - Surrounded himself with advisers from his native Georgia, wouldn’t delegate authority
  - Legislated in secret, generally alienated himself from everyone
    - Fiscal conservative - alienated liberal wing of the Democratic party by refusing to expand social programs or invigorate the economy
    - Alienated Conservatives by pardoning Vietnam War draft evaders, returning control of Panama Canal to Panama, resuming diplomatic relations with China, and halting the construction of new nuclear weapons
- Economy continued to decline
  - Blamed Americans for causing the present ‘malaise’ through loss of moral virtue
- Foreign Policy and the Iran Hostage Crisis
  - Camp David Accords
    - Brought Israel’s Menachem Begnin and Egypt’s Anwar Sadat together to recognize state of Israel in return for regaining control of the Sinai peninsula
  - US-Soviet Relations broke down
    - USSR invaded Afghanistan in 1979
    - Senate never ratified SALT II
    - US boycotted the 1980 Summer Olympics in Moscow
  - Iran government upheaval
    - 1979: followers of Ayatollah Ruhollah Khomeini overthrew Mohammed Reza Pahlevi (the shah of Iran)
      - Shah installed as dictator w/ help of CIA in 1953
      - Followers of Khomeini sought to purge Iran of secular Western influences.
    - Iranian militants seized US embassy in Tehran, taking 66 Americans hostage
      - Demanded that the US return the shah (who fled to NY for medical treatment) and issue an apology
      - Negotiations to free remaining 52 hostages took over a year
      - Complex rescue tatempt failed, killing 8 American soldiers in a helicopter crash
      - US agreed to pay \$8 billion to end the hostage crisis, and hostages were not released until Reagan’s inauguration day

</details>
