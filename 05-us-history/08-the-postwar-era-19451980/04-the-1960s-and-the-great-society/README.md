### Sixties Counterculture (1960–1969)

*Historical Periods · American · [Notion](https://app.notion.com/p/25a44de2a65680e5a173e0328cc28917)*

Rebellion against the fundamental values and structure of American Society, failures of postwar America, rigid social norms, consumer culture, racial injustice, and expanding military industrial complex

**Sources**

- [Khan Outline](https://www.khanacademy.org/humanities/us-history/postwarera/1960s-america/a/the-counterculture-of-the-1960s)

<!-- page-notes:25a44de2a65680e5a173e0328cc28917 -->
#### Page notes

- The Rise of the Counterculture
  - Economic growth after WWII encouraged consumerism and conformity; young Americans questioned these values
  - Influence by the Civil Rights movement, the New Left, and Beat Generation
  - Criticism of US Cold War policies, calling for participatory democracy
  - Feminist movement focusing on gender equality, women’s rights in the workplace, education, reproductive freedom, and challenging traditional gender roles in society
  - Music, art, and literature reflected shifts
    - Musicians included Bob Dylan and the Beatles
    - Woodstock Festival of 1969 a symbol of youth rebellion and communal living
    - Experimentation with alternative lifestyles, communal living, and psychadelic drugs
- Government Responses
  - Lyndon B Johnson’s Great Society Programs
    - Expand access to education and healthcare, addressing poverty
    - Medicare, medicade, and federal funding for public broadcasting were responses to calls for a more just society
  - Suppression by Nixon
    - Campaigned on a law and order campaign, cracking down on anti-war protests and expanding the war on drugs
    - COINTELPRO (FBI) targeted leftist organizations, including anti-war groups, Black activists, and feminists
    - Conservative politicians pushed back against changing sexual norms and family restructures, pushing for a return to traditional values
- Lasting impact
  - Same debates continue today (racial justice, feminism, drug policy, LGBTQ+ rights, role of government in shaping morality)
  - Backlash fueled the rise of conservative movements in the 1970s and beyond; Reagan called for revival of “family values” and limited government intervention in inequality and civil rights issues

### John F Kennedy (1961–1963)

*Historical Figures · American · [Notion](https://app.notion.com/p/23344de2a65680caaf8aebc7063586fc)*

![John F Kennedy](https://upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/c/c3/John_F._Kennedy%2C_White_House_color_photo_portrait.jpg/250px-John_F._Kennedy%2C_White_House_color_photo_portrait.jpg)

US President (Democratic)

Served at height of Cold War, majority of foreign policies wrt Soviet Union and Cuba

Attempted to overthrow the Cuban government of Fidel Castro in the Bay of Pigs Invasion and Operation Mongoose. Resulting tensions termed the Cuban Missile Crisis.

Sent army convoy to reassure West Berliners of US Support following the erection of the Berlin Wall

Signed first nuclear weapons treaty, established the Peace Corps, Alliance for Progress, and continued the Apollo program with goal of landing a man on the moon before 1970

Generally unsuccessful in passing New Frontier domestic policies

Fun facts:  
Presidential debates for this election were the first to be televised

#### Page notes

<details>
<summary>The Election of 1960: Nixon vs JFK</summary>

- Considered the “first modern presidential campaign”
  - First nationally televised presidential debate
  - JFK good on camera, while Nixon sweaty and brooding. Those who listened to dfebate on radio thought Nixon had won, but those who watched on tv agreed Kennedy was the winner
- Kennedy ran on a strong civil rights program
  - Hoped to offset hostility from Democrats by adding Texan Lyndon B Johnson as his VP
- Kennedy clan already well known
- Both Nixon and Kennedy strongly anti-communist; Nixon promised to slash federal spending while Kennedy planned to revive the economy by strengthening the public sector
  - Kennedy was Catholic, and campaign faced accusations that loyalty to the Pope would trump loyalty to US

</details>

<details>
<summary>Office Prioritizations</summary>

- Domestic economic growth
  - Federal tax and spending policies
  - Keynesian Economics: Federal deficit spending could boost economic growth and lower unemployment
  - Stimulus measures to combat the recession: Extension of social security and unemployment benefits, increase in military spending, increase in minimum wage, over \$4 billion allocated for housing construction
  - Policies increased inflation and set stage for conflict between corporations and labor unions over wages and prices
  - Cutting taxes
  - Boosting Federal Spending
- Actual civil rights actions fairly limited. Southern Democrats in Congress threatened to block civil rights agenda.
  - Bill he introduced would be passed as the Civil Rights Act of 1964 following his assassination

</details>

<details>
<summary>Foreign policy</summary>

- Bay of Pigs Invasion
- Cuban Missile Crisis
- Supported military coup in South Vietnam

</details>

- Assassinated in 1963by Lee Harvey Oswald
  - Lee Harvey Oswald: Unstable ex-Marine with ties to the Soviet Union and to the Cuban emigre community in Miami

### Lyndon B Johnson (1963–1969)

*Historical Figures · American · [Notion](https://app.notion.com/p/23344de2a65680768a6bcd2dc34a978d)*

![Lyndon B Johnson](https://upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/c/c4/Lyndon_B._Johnson%2C_photo_portrait%2C_color_%283x4_cropped%29.jpg/250px-Lyndon_B._Johnson%2C_photo_portrait%2C_color_%283x4_cropped%29.jpg)

US President (Democratic)

Assumed presidency following assassination of John F Kennedy

Prioritized containment of communism, including in the ongoing Vietnam War. Became less popular following Vietnam War-related unrest, including race riots

”Great Society” policy to expand civil rights, public broadcasting, healthcare access, education and the arts, urban and rural development, consumer protection, environmentalism, and pulic services. Signed the Social Security Amendments of 1965 resulting in Medicare and Medicate. Made the Apollo program a national priority, enacted the Higher Education Act of 1965 (federally insured student loans), signed Immigration and Nationality Act of 1965 (immigration policy), and expanded civil rights legacy via Civil Rights Act of 1964, Voting Rights Act of 1965, and Civil Rights act of 1968.

Fun facts:  
Won the largest share of the popular vote for the Democratic party in history, and the highest for any candidate since the advent of widespread popular elections in the 1820s

Considered by many historians to be the most effective majority leader

Presidency marked the peak of modern American Liberalism

#### Page notes

<details>
<summary>Overview</summary>

- Ascended to presidency following assassination of JFK; sworn in just 2 hours after the assassination
- Senate Minority Leader for 2 years, Senate Majority Whip for 6 years, and some historians consider him the most effective majority leader in US History

</details>

<details>
<summary>Civil Rights Movement</summary>

- Moved quickly to secure the passage of the legislation that Kennedy introduced
- Civil Rights Act of 1964 banned segregation in public education and facilities and prohibited discrimination in jobs and housing
- In March 1965, delivered a speech in which he condemned racial bigotry and informed the nation he was sending another civil rights bill to Congress
- Voting Rights Act of 1965 outlawed poll taxes, literacy tests, and other devices used to prevent Southern Blacks from voting
- Most comprehensive civil rights legislation ever passed, paramount achievement of Johnson’s presidency

</details>

<details>
<summary>The Great Society</summary>

- A package of programs and legislation aimed at eradicating poverty and improving healthcare and education
- Vastly expanded the welfare state and started the War on Poverty
- Created the Job Corps and the Community Action Program
  - Aimed to eliminate poverty through job creation and block grants to local communities for services like Head Start for early childhood development
- Office of Economic Opportunity established to oversee disbursement of funds to community-based anti-poverty programs
- Food Stamp Act of 1964 to expand federal food stamp program
- Established Medicare and Medicaid, which provide healthcare to the poor and elderly
- Education reform: Primary and Secondary Education Act of 1965 authorized \$1 billion in federal funding for public education and established special programs for schools in low-income areas
- Higher Education Act of 1965 increased federal funding for universities
  - also extended scholarships and low-interest loans to college students
- Expanded far beyond the limits of the New Deal, constitutes one of Johnson’s most enduring legacies

</details>

<details>
<summary>Foreign Policy</summary>

- Vastly expanded US military role in Vietnam
  - August 1964: reports that North Vietnamese torpedo boats attacked 2 US destroyers in the Gulf of Tonkin: Johnson requ3ested and obtained from Congress the Gulf of Tonkin Resolution
    - Authorized the use of military force in Southeast Asia
  - Later came out that the Johnson administration lied about the scope and nature of US involvement in the war; anti-war sentiment intensified and LBJ’s approval ratings plummeted
- Chose not to re-run for election in 1968 due to Vietnam debacle and disarray of Democratic Party

</details>
