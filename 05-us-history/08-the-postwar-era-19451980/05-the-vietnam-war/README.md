### The Anti-War Movement (1964–1980)

*Historical Periods · American · [Notion](https://app.notion.com/p/25a44de2a65680579ffad02526af4f60)*

The student movement arose to demand free speech on college campuses, then pivoted to the US involvement in the Vietnam war, spurred with news coverage of the war and revelations that the Johnson and Nixon administrations lied about the war

**Sources**

- [Khan Outline](https://www.khanacademy.org/humanities/us-history/postwarera/1960s-america/a/the-student-movement-and-the-antiwar-movement)

#### Page notes

<details>
<summary>Origins</summary>

- UC Berkeley: Students protested the university’s sudden attempt to prevent them from organizing politically on campus, starting the **Free Speech Movement**
- Other institutions joined: **Students for a Democratic Society** (Michigan) issued the **Port Huron Statement** which criticized US foreign policy and attacked the Cold War assumptions behind it
- Major part of the **New Left**, a broad political movement challenging authority, embracing sexual liberation and unabashed drug use

*[Image in Notion: Woodstock, August 1969](https://app.notion.com/p/25a44de2a65680579ffad02526af4f60)*

</details>

<details>
<summary>Vietnam and the rise of the antiwar movement</summary>

- 1965 - President Lyndon Johnson escalates US troop presence and bombing in Vietnam; war becomes focal point for student political activism
- Student Groups very active in the effort
  - Held protests and demonstration, burned draft cards
  - “Hey, hey, LBJ, how many kids did you kill today?”
  - Movement grew as US spending on the war grew and economy at home suffered
- 1968 - Nixon campaigns on basis of law-and-order being needed in such a chaotic society; implied harsh approach to dealing with antiwar activists
  - Nixon reduced troop presence and reformed the draft to quash domestic dissent
  - Eliminated draft and replaced with an all-volunteer professional army
  - Authorized FBI and CIA to expand surveillance and harassment of antiwar protest groups
- Role of the Media
  - Investigative journalists able to uncover and reveal the true version of the US war effort, conditions in Southeast Asia, graphic images of death and destruction
  - 1971- NYT released the Pentagon Papers story, a DoD report that concluded that Johnson and Nixon administrations systematically lied to the American people and Congress
  - Watergate scandal involved Nixon’s authoirzation of illegal wiretapping of his political enemies, eroding trust in the president and government

</details>

### Vietnam War (1965–1973)

*Wars · American, Vietnamese · [Notion](https://app.notion.com/p/22344de2a65680c1a0e8e4222b33f44d)*

![Vietnam War](https://images.immediate.co.uk/production/volatile/sites/7/2019/04/GettyImages-2717016-93218c6.jpg)

US & South Vietnam vs North Vietnam & Viet Cong

Preventing spread of communism in Southeast Asia

Effectively disproved “domino theory” logic, which contended that the falling of one country to communism would result in other surrounding counties succumbing to communism

Resulted in “Vietnam Syndrom”, entailing an unwillingness of the American people to become bogged down in foreign wars in which American national security interests were unclear

#### Page notes

<details>
<summary>Context</summary>

- Associated Historical Events
  - Late 1800s: France was colonizing southeast Asia
  - 1885: Vietnam part of French Indonesia
  - 1941: Viet Minh / Ho Chi Minh
  - 1945: “Independence”
  - 1946-1954: FIrst Indochina War
  - 1954: Geneva Conference of 1954/Partition
  - 1955-1963: Ngo Dinh Diem President of South
  - 1964: Gulf of Tonkin Incident
  - 1965: Kiy and Thieu
  - 1968: Tet Offensive, My Lai
  - 1971: Pentagon Papers
  - 1973: Paris Peace Accords
  - 1975: Fall of Saigon
- Liberation movement following WWII leads to Vietnam’s independence: Viet Minh led by Ho Chi Minh (Communist)
- War between the North (Chinese, and those sympathetic to the Vietnamese) and the South (French, British)

</details>

<details>
<summary>Control</summary>

- Ho Chi Minh City was previously Saigon
- Post-WWII, Vietnam partitioned into North Vietnam and South Vietnam for a “cooling off” period before elections; not meant to be a permanent divide. For 300 days, anyone can cross the border.
  - Most of the movement was by Roman Catholic Vietnamese from the north to the south
- Ngo Dinh Diem becomes president of the South
  - Corrupt leader, takes control, and anti-election, but palatable to US because anti-communist and western
  - US supports under the guise of “advisors”
  - Persecuted Buddhists - stormed temples
- Two ongoing conflicts:
  - Ongoing conflict between the North and the South, and forces sympathetic to each side
  - Viet Cong rises up against South — Insurrection
  - Neither a formal war

</details>

<details>
<summary>Public Perception of the War</summary>

- Diem assassinated in 1963; Nguyen Van Thieu takes over (after getting Nguyen Tao Ky out of the picture)
- US sends ships into the Gulf of Tonkin
  - Story claims that the USS Maddox was attacked in the Gulf of Tonkin, and another attack to follow
  - Congress and American People angry that ships were attacked, gave emotional fuel to pass the Gulf of Tonkin resolution, which gave Johnson the authority to engage in Vietnam War
  - Pentagon Papers showed that the Gulf of Tonkin Incidents didn’t really happen like implied
- The Tet Offensive
  - Viet Cong attack on a series of targets along Vietnam
    - Made Americans suspicious - US was told we almost won the war, how can they launch this attack
- My Lai Massacre
  - Documented massacre where US troops decimated a village of innocent women and children; those who tried to defend the villagers were treated similarly
  - Only one of the soldiers who conducted the attack was given jail time, and then only a couple of years
  - Disgusted public when it came out
- Pentagon Papers were leaked and revealed how much the government was lying to Congress and its people
- Ho Chi Minh Trail (network of trails) was carpet bombed, including Laos and Cambodia
- Paris Peace Accords announce peace between the North, South, and US; North then overruns the south and Saigon falls, becoming Ho Chi Minh City
- Nguyen Van Thieu flees with \$15 million and 15 tons of luggage, lives the rest of his life in Massachusetts

</details>

<details>
<summary>Parties</summary>

- Democratic Republic of Vietnam (DRV) in north supported by Soviet Union, China, and other communist countries
- United States and its anticommunist allies backed the Republic of Vietnam (ROV) in the south

### Henry Kissinger (1969–1977)

*Historical Figures · American · [Notion](https://app.notion.com/p/24044de2a65680e291ecc3a39446f9dd)*

![Henry Kissinger](https://upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/9/97/Henry_A._Kissinger%2C_U.S._Secretary_of_State%2C_1973-1977.jpg/250px-Henry_A._Kissinger%2C_U.S._Secretary_of_State%2C_1973-1977.jpg)

Diplomat and Political Scientist.   
Secretary of State and National Security Advisor (Under Nixon and Ford)

Past experience: emigrated as a Jewish refugee fleeing Nazi persecution (1938). Served in US Army for WWII, then attended Harvard. Reputation for expert on nuclear weapons and foreign policy.

Advocate of *Realpolitik (*politics based on facts and standing vs ethics and morals) and pioneered the policy of *detente (strained relations) *with the Soviet Union.

Was awarded Nobel Peace Prize (1973) for his role negotiating the Paris Peace Accords (ending American involvement in the Vietnam War) - controversial for many reasons. Kissinger associated with bombing Cambodia, 1971 Bolivian coup d’etat, 1973 Chilean coup d’etat, support for Argentina’s military junta in its Dirty War, support for Indonesia in its invasion of East Timor, and supported Pakistan during Bangladesh Liberation War and Bangladesh genocide. Criticised for the civilian death toll of the policies he pursued and for his role facilitating US support for authoritarian regimes.
