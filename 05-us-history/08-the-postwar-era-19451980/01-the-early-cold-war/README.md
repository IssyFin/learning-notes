### Cold War (1947–1991)

*Wars · American, Soviet · [Notion](https://app.notion.com/p/24644de2a6568090b4edcf672d2ce8a5)*

A proxy war between the US and the Soviet Union of ideologies (capitalist vs communist), fought in proxy wars (Korean War, Vietnam War) and alliances

**Sources**

- [Khan video on origins](https://www.khanacademy.org/humanities/us-history/postwarera/x71a94f19:the-cold-war-begins/v/origins-of-the-cold-war)

<!-- page-notes:24644de2a6568090b4edcf672d2ce8a5 -->
#### Page notes

<details>
<summary>Origins</summary>

- Cold War: The two major powers never actually fight one another with their own troops; rather, through proxy wars (eg Korean war in 1950s, Vietnam war in 1960s, Cuba, etc) support via funding and troops
- Truman vs Stalin
  - Truman: End of WWII, atomic bomb
  - Stalin: Soviet Union, Security, doesn’t want to be invaded by Germany (both WWI and WWII); wants world to be safe for communism.
    - Best way to make sure of that is to have a buffer zone between Russia and Eastern Europe
      - Lots of these countries “won” from Hitler, and already have boots on the ground
    - Not enough resources to take care of these locations
- Europe in economic trouble following WWII - starvation, damaged infrastructure, no fuel in 1946 terrible winter
  - Bad conditions = fuel for revolution
  - US worried that communism will get a foothold because of communism territory in Europe and bad conditions in Europe
    - In particular, worried Stalin will be Hitler pt 2
    - Appeasement didn’t work; we didn’t confront Hitler head-on, and that didn’t help anything; just meant that he could gain territory
    - US not bombed to cinders, so they have the resources to fight communism
      - Also in a better economic place to support self-determination and comforts
    - Goal is to set up “sandbags” around communist regions

</details>

### The Berlin Airlift and the Creation of NATO (1947–1949)

*Historical Events · American · [Notion](https://app.notion.com/p/24644de2a656807fb1bed90a254d9592)*

Soviet Union blockaded Berlin; the US led a year-long airlift to supply citizens stranded in the western zone of the city

US joined the North Atlantic Treaty Organization (NATO) defensive alliance and ramped up security at home with the National Security Act

Signaled the end of hope for world peace following Hitler’s defeat

**Sources**

- [Khan Academy Outline](https://www.khanacademy.org/humanities/us-history/postwarera/x71a94f19:the-cold-war-begins/a/start-of-the-cold-war-part-3)

<details>
<summary>Berlin Air Lift (1948)</summary>

- Big Four powers continued occupation following Yalta Conference.
- US, France, and UK united their sections, but Soviet Union wanted to keep regions separate to prevent Germans from attacking again.
- Soviet Union cut off all highway and railroad access to Berlin, which fell in its occupation zone, hoping to control all of Berlin
  - Goal was for West Berlin to starve, or for Western Allies to surrender to Soviets’ wishes for Germany
- US, UK, and France couldn’t allow the Soviet Union to hold Berlin hostage; sent food and fuel to Berlin via airlifts each day and instituted counter-blockade on East Berlin
- After 11 months, Soviet blockade was deemed a failure and was ended
  - Soviet Union then turned their region into East Germany, while Western Allies turned their occupation zones, combined, into East Germany
  - Soviets built a wall between eastern and western zones which stood for forty years

</details>

<details>
<summary>NATO and the National Security Act (1948)</summary>

- Tensions continued in Europe; Britain, Belgium, Netherlands, and Luxembourg requested to join defensive alliance
  - US broke longstanding isolationist policy and signed for their inclusion
- NATO promise: an attack on one of its members would provoke a response from all of its members
- Major international body opposing communism in the twentieth century

</details>

<details>
<summary>National Security Act (1947)</summary>

- United branches of armed services under the new Department of Defense
- Created the CIA and the NSA to gather intelligence and advise the president on foreign policy
- Reinstituted the Selective Service military draft for young men in 1948
- Effectively signaled end of hope for world peace following Hitler’s defeat

</details>

### Truman Doctrine and the Marshall Plan (1947–1980)

*Historical Events · American · [Notion](https://app.notion.com/p/24544de2a65680ddb03cca5c1d3394b5)*

![Truman Doctrine and the Marshall Plan](https://cdn.kastatic.org/ka-perseus-images/2bab74a9290c393d8e1e8bd77a4453cf921d5a0e.jpg)

**Truman Doctrine:** The US pledge to help any nation resist communism in order to prevent its spread. A policy of containment that demonstrated the US would not return to isolationism after WWII and instead take an active role in world affairs

**Marshall Plan:** The US pledged \$13 billion to Europe to help rebuild after the war.

**Sources**

- [Khan Academy Outline](https://www.khanacademy.org/humanities/us-history/postwarera/x71a94f19:the-cold-war-begins/a/start-of-the-cold-war-part-2)

<!-- page-notes:24544de2a65680ddb03cca5c1d3394b5 -->
#### Page notes

- Truman Doctrine
  - US wary of communism post Long Telegram and WWII
    - Late 1940s, some governments in Europe and Asia adopted communism
  - UK notificed US that it could no longer afford to fight against communist insurgencies in Greece and Turkey: Truman issued the Truman Doctrine
    - Ended a century and a half of isolationist foreign policy
  - Continued to drive American interventions through the 1980s
- Marshall Plan
  - Post-War reconstruction; crippled infrastructure and massive food shortages in winter of 1946-1947
  - US worried that hungry devastated Europe would turn to communism (as China did in 1949) - supplied Europe with \$13 billion in economic aid
  - Incredibly successful; rehabilitated European nations that accepted the aid, and provided a boost to the American economy (funds were used to purchase American goods)

### Korean War (1950–1953)

*Wars · Korean, American, Chinese · [Notion](https://app.notion.com/p/22344de2a65680a39a95e1e63e7081b3)*

![Korean War](https://upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/f/f0/U.S._Army_machine_gun_team_near_the_Chongchon_River_in_North_Korea_%28November_1950%29.jpg)

***US/UN and South Korea vs North Korea and China. First “Hot War” of the Cold War***

Containment of communism and defense of South Korea

Korea occupied by Japan \~1910-1945 (end of WWII). Forced labor, forced prostitution, attempted eradication of the Korean language.   
1945-Soviets come from above and US comes from below (first occupying Japan). US and Soviet Union identify the 38th parallel as the “dividing” point. North Korea comes under Russian influence, South Korea comes under USA influence.

Soviets install Kim Il Sung (Father of Kim Jong-Il and grandfather of Kim-Jong-Un).  
In South, 1948- attempted elections (seriously rigged) selected Syngman Rhee.

North/Soviets focus on building military, south doesn’t. Both want to unite Korea under their own rule, set up troops along the border w/ skirmishes across the border. China (Mao Zedong) interested in supporting North Korea b/c 1949 China became communist and dislikes the USA.

North invades the South, storming through the peninsula. UN Supports the south, but South Korea so unprepared, US needs to commit ground forces. At the Pusan perimeter (Southeast South Korea), able to hold their own, build up significant troops, and push back. Douglas MacArthur (under Truman, who has trouble keeping MacArthur in check) takes over in the South, overstepping his bounds. Becomes a back-and-forth of spreading forces thin and rebuilding. Amphibious landing at Inchan (near Seoul) allows South to disrupt North supply line.

China secretly builds up troops along river (Yalu) and recapture Seoul. US then recaptures Seoul, and MacArthur tries to get permissions to use nuclear weapons on China (then dismissed by Truman).

Back to square 1 at 38th parallel. Armistice signed after 2 years of negotiations. (note - armistice isn’t a peace treaty or formal end to war; technically still in a state of war) .

Lots of atrocities on both sides.

### Dwight D Eisenhower (1953–1961)

*Historical Figures · American · [Notion](https://app.notion.com/p/23344de2a65680cfb2f9d6eae20302c7)*

![Dwight D Eisenhower](https://upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/0/02/Dwight_D._Eisenhower%2C_official_photo_portrait%2C_May_29%2C_1959_%28cropped%29%283%29.jpg/250px-Dwight_D._Eisenhower%2C_official_photo_portrait%2C_May_29%2C_1959_%28cropped%29%283%29.jpg)

US President (Republican)

Originally entered the presidential race to prevent Taft’s isolationist policies

Primary goals were to prevent spread of communism and reduce the national deficit. ”New Look” Policy of nuclear deterrence; prioritized “inexpensive” nuclear weapons while reducing funding for military activities

Recognized Taiwan as the legitimate government of China, considered using nuclear weapons to end the Korean War and may have threatened China with a nuclear attack if an armistice was not reached quickly. Otherwise avoided conflict; suspended nuclear atmospheric testing. But continued Cold-War policies in “us-vs-them” mindset and supported dictatorships that supported their own interests. Deposed the democratically elected leader of Guatemala. “New Look” meant paying a lot for nuclear weapons to project strength without needing to actually “fight”. Resulted in overarching fear of nuclear war

Considered a “moderate republican”. Didn’t try to dismantle the social welfare programs of the New Deal, and pushed the interstate highway. Deployed troops to Little Rock to enforce the desegregation policy. Union membership reached a historic peak. Signed the Civil Rights Act in an aim to stop people from being denied their right to vote.

Fun facts:  
First supreme commander of NATO

### Bay of Pigs Invasion (1961)

*Historical Events · American, Cuban · [Notion](https://app.notion.com/p/24244de2a6568026b512dcd9ad8efe5a)*

US Leads a poorly-planned invasion on Cuba following a revolution by communist-like Castro. Results in strengthening of Cuba-Soviet relations, embarrassment of the US, loss of resources, and the Cuban Missile Crisis.

  - JFK stated the US had a part in Cuba conditions during the Batista regime: “*I believe that there is no country in the world including any and all the countries under colonial domination, where economic colonization, humiliation, and exploitation were* <u>***worse than in Cuba, in part owing to my country’s policies during the Batista regime***</u>*… to some extent it is as though Batista was the incarnation of a number of sins on the part of the United States. Now we shall have to pay for those sins*.” (JFK, 1963)
- 1959-Revolution in Cuba, led by Fidel Castro (and right-hand men Raul Castro and Che Guevara)
  - Start taking over lands that were previously private property, US property, and generally ill-gotten land during Batista’s regime.
    - Why people considered it a communist revolution rather than nationalist revolution
  - Resulted in middle-class and upper-middle-class Cubans fleeing to US (Florida), afraid of property stolen during the revolution

- Director of CIA, Allen Dulles, plans to oust Fidel Castro in a way that doesn’t look like US is responsible:
  - 1400 exiled Cubans sign up for a CIA-backed, US-backed force to invade Cuba and overthrow Fidel Castro

  - Invasion a complete debacle and failure. CIA blamed JFK for not providing enough air support/resources, JFK blamed CIA for poor planning and misinformation
    - Strengthened Fidel Castro’s hold on Cuba: led to him being openly communist (previously trying to stay on US good side) and demonstrate that they got the best of the US’s best effort.
    - Led to the Cuban missile crisis

### The Cuban Missile Crisis (1962)

*Historical Events · American, Soviet · [Notion](https://app.notion.com/p/25644de2a65680ba8d65dbe697803b86)*

The closest that the US and Soviet Union ever came to outright nuclear war; a 2-week period of bluffs and chicken between JFK and Soviet Premier Nikita Khrushchev

**Sources**

- [Khan Outline
- Khan Video](https://www.khanacademy.org/humanities/us-history/postwarera/x71a94f19:the-cuban-missile-crisis/v/cuban-missile-crisis)

<!-- page-notes:25644de2a65680ba8d65dbe697803b86 -->
#### Page notes

<details>
<summary>Context</summary>

- Cuba just had a revolution
- Cold War Strategy of containment; US wants Cuba to *not* be communist
  - US supports/leads Bay of Pigs Invasion, which doesn’t go well
- The US starts placing intermediate-range missiles in Turkey and Italy, which can reach the Soviet Union in \~20 minutes; notion of “if you can hit them fast enough, they won’t be able to retaliate”. We didn’t want Soviet Union to do this to us.

</details>

<details>
<summary>US sees missiles in Cuba</summary>

- US concerned that Soviet Union has close-range ballistic missiles in Cuba that the could use on the US in a short time frame
- JFK holds a meeting of the EXCOMM (Executive Committee) to figure out what to do about this crisis
- Soviets (led by Krushchev) consider US leadership inexperienced and weak - more willing to play “chicken”

</details>

<details>
<summary>US Response</summary>

- Considered blockade, but would look like an act of war
- Considered invading Cuba (would also look like an act of war)
  - JFK’s committee tried to encourage him to launch an invasion
- Soviet Union considered their weapons to be defensive weapons - and surface-to-surface generally doesn’t imply defensive
- JFK starts a quarantine on all offensive military equipment; doesn’t do a full blockade (block of the necessities of life) but just anything that can make weapons
  - Soviet Union responds that they see this as an act of aggression - game of chicken
- Whole world is holding their breath and ,aking statements, JFK tries to soften things without backing down (look, we had to do this, can ou work with us?), Soviet Union says they’re trying to defend ally (Castro) who US is trying to oust, and pointed out missiles in Italy and Turkey
  - Soviets request demobilization and guarantee they wouldn’t attack Cuba
  - Soviet second telegram requests removal of missiles in Italy and Turkey
  - U2 meanwhile shot down by Cuba
- US only responds to first telegram, and says “understanding” on Turkey missiles situation (without firm promises
  - Missiles in Italy and Turkey were already obsolete, since we had submarines with missiles
  - Second agreement done secretly so US doesn’t look weak
- Note that throughout the negotiations, Khrushchev failed to negotiate with Castro
- 13Days Book discusses this period

</details>
