# Hypertension

_Health › Circulatory system diseases_

[Course link](https://www.khanacademy.org/science/health-and-medicine/circulatory-system-diseases)

## Summary

- Hypertension: High blood pressure
- Hypertension is considered the silent killer; no warning signs or symptoms, but results in accumulating damage which cause other health problems
- With hypertension, heart has to work harder, and heart and blood vessels are at risk of damage. Heart can become enlarged or can fail. Vessels may bulge, burst, or clog.

## Risk Factors

Hypertension affects 1 in 3 adults worldwide, and contributes to half of all deaths due to heart attack and stroke. Risk factors include: 

- Genetics
- Age (vessels become more rigid with age)
- - Men: Middle-Age (45 years + )
  - Women: Late-middle-age (65 years + )
- Obesity
- Excessive alcohol consumption
- Smoking
- Certain medications
- Vitamin D deficiency

## Diagnosis, Prevention, and Treatment

### Diagnosis

- Typically diagnosed via a blood pressure reading; may need to take several readings over the course of a day
- Blood test to identify source of high blood pressure:
- - Detect organ damage
  - Identify cause of secondary hypertension
  - Blood Urea Nitrogen (BUN) and Creatinine to determine kidney function
  - - If kidneys aren't working, urea won't be deposited, and urea levels will be much higher in the blood
    - Creatinine is another waste product, released by muscle decomposition; generally fairly level, but builds up if kidneys aren't functioning well
  - Glucocorticoids: Cushings Syndrome (high levels of cortisole for an extended period of time)
  - Blood Glucose: Diabetes
  - Lipids: Hypertension and heart disease
- Electrocardiogram:
  - Records heart's electrical activity; typically tall R wave indicates left ventricular hypertrophy
  - Paired with a chest x-ray

### Prevention

- Reduce salt intake
- Eat a healthy diet
- - Reduce high-fat and calorie lden foods
  - Add fruits and vegetables
- Increase amount of exercise
- Quit smoking
- Reduce daily alcohol to 1 (women) or 2 (men) drinks per day.
- Monitor blood pressure with 2-3 yearly readings

### Treatment

Treatment includes prevention methods (lifestyle changes) but may also include the following:

- Diurtics: Promote production of urine to reduce fluid in body, immediately reducing blood pressure
- Beta-blockers: Slows beating of heart and reduces force, and opens up blood vessels
- Angiotensin-converting enzyme inhibitors (ACE inhibitors): Blocks hormones which constrict, thicken, and stiffen blood vessels and heart, triggers release of hormones that increase sodium and water in body
- Angiotensin II receptor blockers: Work similarly to ACE inhibitors
- Alpha Blockers: Block action of hormones triggering vasoconstriction of smaller arteries and veins
- Calcium Channel BLockers: Relax and widen blood vessels by preventing calcium from entering heart cells; slows heart rate

## Blood Pressure Scale

| Systolic | Diastolic | Category |
| <90 mm | <60 mm | Hypotension | 
| <120 mm | <80 mm | Normal Blood Pressure |
| 120-139 mm | 80-89 mm | Prehypertension | 
| 140-159 mm | 90-99 mm | Stage 1 Hypertension | 
| 160+ mm | 100+ mm | Stage 2 Hypertension | 

Note that, if a reading covers multiple categories, the higher category (eg Stage 2 over Stage 1) takes precedent.

## Causes of hypertension

- Increased flow: increased pressure, triggered from kidneys (typically due to excess of salt)
- Increased resistance: Reduction in vessel diameter due to plaque or constriction, stiffening of vessels

## Effects of hypertension

### On Blood Vessels

- Vessels can become more rigid
- Vessels can develop an aneurism (bulge in vessel)
- Burst vessels can result in organ damage; often kidney damage, retinopathy
- Shear stress of blood vessels increases with pressure; endothelium takes damage and expands outward; microscopic tears in endothelium which scar and become rigid and more able to catch flowing particles (atherosclerotic plaque)

### On the Heart

- Heart becomes enlarged to move the blood:
  - Left Ventricular Hypertrophy (strengthening of the heart, uses more oxygen and energy but doesn't have a way to get it - can result in a heart attack)
- Heart can't generate enough pressure - heart failure
- Extra muscle to account for pressure; filling volume decreases and pump is less effective - early manifestation of heart failure

### Complications

- Head and Brain
- - Hypertensive Retinopathy: damage to retina, results in vision problems
  - Stroke and brain death due to lack of blood
- Heart
- - Heart failure
  - Left ventricular hypertrophy (muscle growth); more work, need to pump harder, decrease in pumpking ability
  - Coronary Artery Disease and heart attacks
- Peripheral Artery Disease
- - Atherosclerosis anywhere else (frequently legs, arms, stomach, kidneys)
  - Kidney damage, leading to even more difficulty controlling blood pressure

## Types of Hypertension

### Primary Hypertension

- "Essential" Hypertension
- Caused by something indirect (eg smoking, obesity, etc)
- Accounts for most cases of hypertension (>90%)

### Secondary Hypertension

- Caused by something direct (eg kidney disease)

### Malignant Hypertension

- Rapid rise in blood pressure with damage to heart and blood vessels
- Tends to be >240/120

### Hypertensive Crisis

- Typically, slow rise in blood pressure means symptoms aren't detected
- Hypertensive crisis is a rapid rise in blood pressure which typically produces immediate symptoms
- Hypertensive Urgency: No target organ damage
- - over 180 mmHg (systolic) or 110 mmHg (diastolic)
  - Symptoms: Headaches, shortness of breath, nosebleed, severe anxiety
- Hypertensive Emergency/Malignant Hypertension: Target organ damage
- - over 180 mmHg (systolic) or >120 mmHg (diastolic) but *can* be lower
  - Symptoms: chest pain, shortness of breath, back pain, numbness/weakness, vision change, difficulty speaking
  - Example: Encephalopathy: high arterial pressure results in cerebral arterioles inability to regulate blood flow in brain - leaks into interstitial space causing fluid buildup in the brain and skull
