# Atherosclerosis and arteriosclerosis

_Health › Circulatory system diseases_

[Course link](https://www.khanacademy.org/science/health-and-medicine/circulatory-system-diseases)

## Summary

Atherosclerosis (and arteriolosclerosis and atherosclerosis) are the result of the hardening of blood vessels due to plaque. This can cause heart damage, high blood pressure, and other complications.

## Key ideas

- Arteriosclerosis, arteriolosclerosis, and atherosclerosis occur when irritants (commonly streaks of fat) build up in the blood vessels and are covered by plaque, effectively scabbed over.
- Symptoms: Generally minimal until an artery is so affected that blood can't be delivered to organs and tissues, heart attack, and stroke. Can be indicated by angina, Transient Ischemic Attack (TIA), leg pain when walking (claudication/peripheral artery disease), high blood pressure, or kidney failure.
- Causes: Damage to the artery inner layer, due to high blood pressure, high cholesterol, high triglycerides, smoking/tobacco use, diabetes, insulin resistance, obesity, inflammation
- Unchangeable Risk Factors: Aging, Family History of early heart disease or stroke, Inflammatory conditions
- Behavioral Risk Factors: Unhealthy Diet, Diabetes, High Blood Pressure, High Cholesterol, Lack of Exercise, Obsesity, Sleep Apnea, Smoking/Tobacco Use
- Resultant Complications: Coronary artery disease, Carotid artery disease and TIA, Peripheral Artery Disease and Gangrene, Aneurisms, Chronic Kidney Disease

## Terminology

- Arteriosclerosis: A cardiac ailment that results when arteries become thick and stiff.
- Arteriolosclerosis: A cardiac ailment that results when arterioles (small arteries) become thick and stiff
- Atherosclerosis: A type of arteriosclerosis; the buildup of fats, cholesterol, and other substances on artery walls (plaque)
